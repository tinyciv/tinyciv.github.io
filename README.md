# TinyCiv

A 2D RTS in TypeScript with single-player worlds and online 1v1 matches. Control a leader, grow a settlement, build infrastructure, and unite neighboring factions by diplomacy or conquest.

[Play TinyCiv](https://tinyciv.github.io/). The main menu offers single player, multiplayer, camera options and controls. All interface and world text uses Pixel Operator.

## Run

Requires Node.js 22.12+ and npm. GitHub Actions and the multiplayer server use Node 22.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Open the local URL printed by Vite. For a production build:

```sh
npm run build
npm run preview
```

## Multiplayer

Choose **Multiplayer**, enter a player name and faction color, and create a game. A second player selects that game and joins. Both players check **Ready**, then the host chooses **Start match**. Each player controls a separate faction with equal starting resources and forces; the two realms start at war. Capturing or destroying the opposing faction’s last settlement wins the duel. A fallen Leader returns after ten real seconds while a settlement survives. There are no AI-controlled rival factions or neutral villages in this mode.

The AWS server owns the simulation, economy, combat, and command validation. Online matches run at normal speed; opening a menu does not pause the opponent. A disconnected player has 60 seconds to reconnect to their faction while the match pauses. Leaving forfeits the match. Multiplayer never overwrites the single-player save.

To run the multiplayer server locally with Node 22 or newer:

```sh
npm run build:server
PORT=8787 npm run server
```

Open the Vite development URL with `?multiplayer=http://127.0.0.1:8787`. The public AWS endpoint is configured in `.env.production`; production builds otherwise use the current site's origin. Local server origins default to `http://127.0.0.1:5173` and `http://localhost:5173`; set `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` for other ports. Run `npm run test:multiplayer` against a local server, or set `MULTIPLAYER_URL` to the HTTPS deployment URL. `npm run test:multiplayer:browser` verifies the public site using two fresh browser sessions; set `GAME_URL` to test another frontend. Browser screenshots and results go to `output/multiplayer-browser/`. Deployment, rollback, capacity, and operations are documented in [infra/README.md](infra/README.md).

On touch screens, use landscape orientation. The left joystick moves the Leader; tap ground or enemies to issue orders, and tap nearby buildings for their activity panels. Two fingers pan and pinch to zoom. Steering buttons appear for boats and formations. Linear construction uses two taps, while Clear and Forbid retain rectangular dragging.

Leader skill points also unlock three one-time, technology-gated abilities beneath the basic skills: **Engineer** (Metal Tools), **Brace** (Military Tactics), and **Discretion** (Exploration). Death costs one level and 200 gold; surviving settlements automatically restore the Leader after ten real seconds, independent of speed or pause. Defeating a hostile faction Leader grants the killer’s Leader one level and 200 gold; a marauder Leader awards one level and 50 gold.

A completed Scouts’ Lodge unlocks **Observation posts** under Fortify. Each costs 20 gold and one resident, with 3 gold upkeep per 30 seconds. Posts occupy neutral ground, provide 3× sight, and remain concealed until a hostile Leader enters the tile. A discovered scout retreats when attacked and restores the resident on reaching a friendly city. Occasional displaced smoke gives opponents a rough clue.

The Forged Citadel HUD uses carved brass, dark slate, and a circular minimap at bottom left. The bottom contains only the minimap, ten numbered slots, and four persistent actions: **Build (B)**, **Fortify (F)**, **Clear (C)**, and **Forbid**. Slot 1 recalls the retinue and slot 2 toggles its stance using approved icon pair 2B. Their small labels stay on one line. Clicking or using a shortcut flashes the button gold and starts a one-second radial cooldown; each action recovers independently, even while paused. Accepted actions also flash their matching icon above the Leader for two seconds. Change Stance shows the new stance beneath the icon; the popup follows the Leader and camera, and a later action replaces it. Slot 3 toggles the tight rectangular Formation and is locked until Infantry Drill is researched. Slot 4 toggles Raise Mass levy / Dismiss levy after research, and slot 5 toggles Garrison / Recall Garrison from the start; slots 6–10 remain empty. The slots are shorter than the main action buttons, with plain numbers and no separate number boxes. The minimap fits discovered land inside its circular viewport, shows current political boundaries through fog, and supports camera clicks and +/− controls for zooming the world view in and out. Zoom controls dim at their limits; Home centers the camera on the Leader.

The top bar shows resource icons and totals, with rounded changes such as (+30) beside food, gold, and culture. These track actual income received over the previous 30 game seconds, excluding purchases, consumption, upkeep, and refunds. After loading a game, the counters build their history from zero; paused time does not age the window. Additional details remain in tooltips. **Menu** sits immediately left of pause and playback speed. A compact card at the top right shows the selected unit or building, its portrait, HP, and a short health bar. Units and fortification buildings also show Attack and Defense; other buildings hide those rows. Additional details are available in its tooltip. Retinue stance and recall also remain available on **Q** and **E**.

Hover or keyboard-focus the gold tally to see **inputs, outlays and net change over the last 60 game seconds**. The breakdown records actual taxes, trade, loot, treasure, refunds and transfers, alongside construction, upkeep, Science, upgrades, immigration, diplomacy and theft. Refunds count as returned gold, without producing Science or increasing the separate 30-second income counter. This transaction history is saved with the faction; opening the tooltip does not start or reset its window. Paused time does not age it, and displayed amounts are rounded to whole gold.

Build opens a titleless, three-column text popup with sixteen choices, including the Supply depot. Fortify opens palisades, walls, wooden towers, guard posts, ballistae, and Forts in the same popup. Each button puts its keyboard shortcut at the upper right; prices, requirements, and construction times appear on hover. B and F switch or toggle menus; the × button and Esc close them. Opening menus preserves the inspected entity. The four main actions stay visible, and unavailable choices are disabled. On narrow screens the numbered slots occupy a row above the minimap and action buttons. The earlier mob / travel toggle remains on Shift; the researched rectangular Formation uses slot 3. Movement keys work during placement. Clicking empty ground clears selection. Pressing **B–G** opens farm placement. Farms and houses also expand automatically when their settlement’s Governor permissions allow them.

The Leader's blue XP bar sits directly above the numbered slots. Leveling up updates the gold skill-point badge without opening the skill panel or taking focus. Click the badge or XP bar to toggle the compact skill panel. Enemy combat kills by the Leader earn 20 XP; retinue kills share 10% with the Leader (2 XP) while retaining their squad veterancy. Each level requires the current level × 800 XP and grants +10 HP, +1 base Attack, and one skill point. **Power** adds +2 base Attack, **Guard** adds +1 base Defense, and **Command** adds one retinue slot, each with three ranks. The panel previews the actual stat change after equipment and veterancy. Points and bonuses are saved and synchronized in multiplayer; a replacement Leader starts at level 1.

## Construction and movement

Build and mark trees for clearing within ten tiles of the Leader, including entirely unexplored fog. Fog never blocks single-cell or rectangular clearing orders, construction previews, or placement; normal terrain, borders, occupancy, and worker-route rules still apply. **Farms, roads, bridges, irrigation, walls, palisades, ballistae, and new settlements may be built outside borders**, including automatic farms, manually ordered roads, and caravan roads. Ordinary travel creates roads only inside the moving faction’s own borders. Construction workers, harvesters, Leaders, and their retinues never contribute to road wear. Merchant and foot-traffic roads relocate occupied farm cells for free; irrigation ditches can remain beneath roads. Other buildings, including towers, guard posts, Forts, and monuments, require their entire footprint inside your faction’s borders. Neutral-village territory remains reserved against foreign construction. Settlements require a clear 2×2 footprint outside other factions’ borders and a builder route from an existing city. Nearby foreign buildings impose no extra exclusion radius on construction. Automatic farms treat clearable forest as available ground and clear the crop tile when reserved. Site scoring uses terrain-weighted walking distance from usable settlement doors, to each crop tile. Water detours, bridges, roads, fords, cliffs and gates affect the score; a nearby plot across water can lose to a longer straight-line site with a shorter delivery route. Expected surviving harvest, including irrigation, guides placement. New fields favor neighboring crops and fill gaps to form compact blocks, allowing up to a 10% siting preference without increasing actual yield. Relocated crops also favor nearby field blocks, with at most 1.5 extra walking tiles; housing redevelopment still moves farms farther from their settlement. Within six tiles of the settlement, automatic farms prefer plots away from roads and their immediate frontage, with roads excluded from farm placement. Farms need one valid, accessible tile; road connections, terrain, spacing, and builder access still apply. Initial settlements and raider camps are seeded separately. Buildings may stand directly beside walls or palisades. There is no settlement setback; builder access and lanes between solid residential buildings still apply. Walkable farms can touch one another and other buildings. New orders reserve a footprint with sparse stakes. A peasant departs from the nearest reachable completed settlement of the same faction and walks to a working side before construction starts. Distance follows walking cost, including roads, terrain and detours; blocked or forbidden settlement exits are skipped. Queued, paused, and uncleared sites remain open ground for movement; only active solid sites block passage. Units do not prevent placement and move aside before work starts. If they cannot leave, work waits. Completed builders return home, and abandoned work can resume when a replacement arrives.

Roads and farms never share a cell. A new road relocates the farm to a suitable reachable plot at no cost, preserving crop quantities and harvest deadlines. If no replacement plot exists, the road is not placed. Farms cannot be ordered on existing roads. Solid buildings such as libraries can replace friendly farmland: every crop under the full footprint moves to a separate reachable plot for free, retaining harvests and unfinished work. If any farm cannot move, placement leaves all farms and gold unchanged. Other solid buildings can replace roads when their placement rules are satisfied. Roads remain usable until work starts; wall-and-road gate layers remain together. **Clear (C)** uses one rectangular selection for trees, construction sites, and completed buildings. It queues eligible trees for workers, cancels friendly unfinished construction with unused gold refunded, and demolishes completed owned buildings or property inside your borders without a refund. Foreign unfinished property inside your borders can be removed but never yields a refund. You can also drag the Clear button directly onto a single tile. Unsafe occupied crossings are kept, and a failed cancellation never falls through to demolition. Demolishing the last settlement causes defeat. The unbuilt portion of cancelled construction’s paid gold is refunded, including the full price before work starts, and its builder is released. Cancelling an order before work starts preserves roads beneath it. Automatic expansion waits thirty game seconds before retrying a cancelled farm or house; manual orders remain available. A gate's road can be cancelled once any occupants have a reachable space to step aside. All construction sites show four static wooden pegs around their footprint until completion. A small circle in the center shows remaining work as a shrinking pie; it stays full before work begins and freezes when work pauses. The terrain stays visible without scaffolding, lumber, or linear progress bars.

**Forbid** on the main action card designates a rectangular exclusion zone by dragging. Friendly workers, merchants and guards avoid it for travel and work; Leaders and their retinues, including scouts, may cross normally. Existing work is paused or reassigned without destroying the site. Units already inside leave safely. Designations are free and can be made anywhere, including fog. Start a drag inside a forbidden zone to erase that rectangle. Touching zones share one gently hopping white forbidden marker; areas and markers remain visible through fog without revealing the ground beneath. Zones persist in saves.

Each settlement supports **one builder per five residents**, retaining one builder while it has any population. Construction and repairs use the same workers; construction takes priority. Builders and rotating farm couriers share that city's population budget. Civilian deaths remove residents from their home city; a depopulated settlement produces no replacement workers or natural growth until residents return. Civilians flee from visible, reachable hostile units within **two tiles**, or immediately when hit. Initial panic lasts **three seconds**; continued threats extend the deadline to **two seconds** after the latest detection. Morale then recovers at 15 points per second, with 50 needed to resume work. Threatened civilians reach a settlement entrance and hide inside until its exits are safe; sheltered civilians still check threats within seven tiles of accessible entrances. A damaged completed building becomes repairable after **five seconds without being hit**. A peasant travels to it and restores health at **maximum health divided by its original construction time** per second; another hit suspends repairs until the quiet period passes again.

A **house and a well of the same faction may share one tile**, in either build order, including queued construction. Wells may also stand alone. The pair retains separate construction, health and selection; canceling or losing one leaves the other in place. Click the well's front-left artwork to select it, or the house entrance to select the house. Normal costs, well population requirements, house block limits, borders and builder access still apply. A shared well uses the native narrow Puny sprite at its original pixel scale; its small visual overhang does not reserve another tile.

Road, wall, and bridge drags validate each tile independently. Only invalid cells are red; valid, affordable cells are placed and charged. Existing matching segments are skipped. Farm previews reserve one tile. Starting buildings are complete, and traffic roads appear immediately. **Gold is the only construction currency; there is no timber/material resource.**

Completed bridges cross rivers, including fords, at normal movement speed. Solid walls block all units. Placing a road on an existing friendly or neutral wall creates a gate without requiring an adjoining road and bypasses junction and 2×2-road-block rules. In either build order, gates require one intervening wall tile (centers at least two tiles apart); gates cannot touch, including diagonally. Walls and palisades cannot complete a solid 2×2 block. Straight runs, corners, and T junctions remain valid; four-way crosses are rejected, including a new arm that would turn a neighboring T into a cross. Both materials, gate walls, and unfinished orders count together. A road and wall on the same tile form a gate for the wall owner's units and confederated allies; enemies must breach it. Either layer can be ordered first, including a wall over a queued road or a road over a wall. A pending road already provides the gate opening through an active or completed wall, but its road speed bonus requires completion. Ordinary road placement requires connection to an existing road, bridge, or natural ford; wall-backed gate roads can start independently. Farmland remains passable and immune to attacks. New roads and house redevelopment relocate the farm before occupying its cell. Sites provide no housing, crops, or sight until complete.

A bare, dry tile inside the moving faction’s borders becomes a road after **12 unit entries within the past 180 game seconds**, provided it has an orthogonally adjacent completed road, bridge, or natural ford. Manual roads can extend an existing road or bridge, start on either bank of a ford, or begin at an existing friendly or neutral wall. Road drags may begin on or cross natural ford tiles; those crossing tiles are left intact and cost no gold. A wall on a ford can receive a paid road layer to open a gate, retaining the ford’s movement speed. Fords retain their 45% crossing speed unless a bridge is built. Unconnected worn trails wait for the road network to reach them. Construction workers, harvesters, Leaders, warriors, archers, and scouts in faction retinues do not contribute road wear. They cross fields without paving or relocating them; independent village soldiers and guard-post traffic retain ordinary road wear. Standing still does not count. Vegetation, water, and solid building footprints cannot become traffic roads; eligible crop cells are relocated before other traffic or caravans pave them. Ordinary new roads cannot complete a solid 2×2 block, keeping paths as lines and junctions. Road junction centers must be at least two tiles apart, including junctions created on neighboring cells; manual, dragged, caravan, and traffic roads share this rule, except roads placed directly on existing walls. Adjacent roads/bridges and adjacent walls connect, and bank roads join the ford crossing. Roads never create arms into settlement or house footprints. Roads use Puny dirt pathway pixels at 70% of their previous width. They improve travel speed, including harvest delivery, without adding a separate food bonus.

**Village dirt trails** are generated terrain overlays connecting most reachable neutral villages. The network can pass through village streets and uses complete bank-to-bank fords across rivers up to four tiles wide; it never converts open sea or lakes into land. Trails use the narrow, darker native pathway artwork and provide **5% faster movement** relative to the underlying terrain. They cannot be built. Normal manual, caravan, traffic and settlement-ring roads replace the trail beneath them. Unreachable islands may remain disconnected.

Wooden towers have **600 health and 6 armor**, throw javelins at **range 6** with **12.5 base damage** (half the leader's base damage), and provide 12 tiles of sight. Their sprites extend above their collision cells. Automatic farms slightly favor existing building rows and columns. Separate farms can touch on every side without a gap. Buildings use the original Puny World sprites at their native two-world-pixels-per-source-pixel scale, except the blacksmith, which uses the native one-cell Miniworld wood workshop. Only existing Puny color regions, such as roofs and tent fabric, follow each faction’s color. Walls, farms and other neutral structures retain their source colors; no flags are added. Two adjacent native 1×2 buildings form the settlement’s 2×2 footprint. Buildings sit on feathered tan clearings clipped to shorelines without changing terrain or movement costs.

Each guard-post popup includes **Stay inside city walls**, off by default. Enable it to confine that post’s guards to the completed enclosure containing its city and post, including routes and combat positions. Guards already outside return home before resuming defense. They ignore outside chases and civilian rescues, but still attack enemies that enter the enclosure. If the walls are breached or no enclosing perimeter exists, they return to the post and remain inside until it is enclosed again. Other posts keep their own settings.

**Wooden palisades** are available immediately in **Fortify → Palisade (F, then O)**. Each timber tile costs **5 gold**, takes four seconds of work, and has **56.25 health**. Stone walls cost **30 gold per tile**, have **2,250 health** and **3 base armor**; stone gates retain the same armor with **1,125 health**. A tower mounted on a completed friendly stone wall gains **10× health and 50% more armor**, returning to its ordinary statistics if its support is lost. Research and equipment still apply, and layout changes preserve the fraction of health remaining. Stone walls may replace friendly or neutral palisades for **25 gold per tile**, crediting the palisade’s 5-gold value. Drag previews add the actual cost of each new or upgraded tile. The old palisade remains until stone construction finishes; canceling the replacement refunds only unused upgrade spending and leaves the original defense intact. Existing gates and attached towers are preserved. They use the approved Puny World Medieval wooden wall set, with native corners and gate frames, and share stone-wall road gates, ownership and movement rules. Placing either wall type over a friendly farm relocates its crop tile to a suitable reachable plot at no additional cost. The move preserves harvest deadlines and carried food; a drag reserves the complete planned wall so farms are not moved onto it or cut off behind it. If no suitable plot exists, that placement is rejected without moving the farm or spending gold.

A wooden tower and a same-faction wall can share one tile in either build order, including queued construction. Each keeps its normal cost, health, repair and construction job. Click the upper tower or lower wall face to select each separately; during tower construction, click the upper half of the site. Canceling or destroying one leaves the other in place, and the tile remains blocked while either solid layer survives. Gates cannot receive towers. On a completed wall or palisade, wooden towers retain their roof and platform with shortened timber supports so the wall face remains visible. Standalone towers and towers above unfinished walls retain their full legs.

All units use terrain-weighted, eight-direction pathfinding. Cached navigation regions account for faction-specific gates and active construction, and invalidate when access changes. Searches consider travel time and congestion, retry blocked routes, and allow diagonal passage between touching impassable corners. Friendly units clear chains of blockers, prioritizing the leader and working couriers, then resume their tasks. Combatants reserve distinct reachable contact cells; reinforcements route around engaged ranks or choose another target when no contact cell is available. Six varied arrangements of green-and-tan Puny stones mark slow but passable river crossings at the terrain’s native pixel scale. Crossings have a complete bank-to-bank path with irregular, tapered side shallows one to three cells wide. The [new ford variation preview](scratch/2026-10-28-ford-variations/standalone.html) shows all six arrangements. Figures are **30% smaller**. Military units retain a full-cell footprint. Non-military units can pass through one another regardless of faction. Workers, peasants, and merchants also pass through soldiers. Terrain and solid buildings still restrict their paths. Retinue warriors move at **1.1× the leader’s base speed**. In Travel mode, escorts form ranks behind a moving Leader. When the Leader stops or only shuffles locally, escorts gather in a compact group on reachable ground around the Leader; Aggressive escorts move to engage nearby threats instead of waiting at the back of the column. The rank narrows into a single-file column along the leader’s actual path at gates and narrow crossings, then reforms after one second of clear width. Larger retinues use extra ranks on open ground; a long column can extend beyond four tiles in a choke point. New recruits keep existing soldiers’ slots stable. New and replacement warriors spawn one at a time at a completed settlement, at least ten game seconds apart, and travel to the Leader before adopting the current retinue stance. The player Leader and retinue can nudge nonhostile village and peaceful faction military units into nearby open ground to clear a path, without changing their orders. They cannot push hostile units or wildlife, or move blockers through water, solid buildings, or forbidden cells. The player Leader passes through friendly units without collision. Outside Infantry Drill formation, retinue soldiers still collide with friendly military units, including the Leader, and use yielding to get around them. Selecting a warrior leaves its order unchanged. Formation positions respect reachable terrain and occupied cells. Routing adds a 5% movement penalty.

Press **2 / Q**, or click slot 2, to cycle the entire retinue through **Defensive**, **Hold**, and **Aggressive**. Defensive warriors can fight and move within three tiles of the position where the stance was issued. Hold keeps them at their posts. Aggressive warriors pursue enemies in sight and follow the Leader when there is no target. A right-click attack overrides these stances temporarily; an unreachable target restores the previous activity.

Press **Shift** to toggle **Formation** and **Travel**. Formation gathers the retinue into a compact mob with the Leader at its center. The Leader moves at three-quarter speed. Warriors use normal speed to reach changing slots, then match the formation's pace. A clustered shield or foot icon appears briefly above the Leader. Escorts can leave the mob to engage enemies, then rejoin.

**Infantry Drill** follows Military Tactics. It unlocks **Formation (3)**: close rectangular ranks centered on the Leader, with a **1.6:1 width-to-depth ratio** and the wider side across the forward direction. Ranks remain balanced as the retinue grows; ranged troops occupy the rear. Forming ranks requires **eight living eligible retinue soldiers**, excluding the Leader. An active formation can continue with five to seven soldiers; falling below five immediately disbands it and routes the surviving participants and Leader with zero morale. A/D shift sideways without turning; W/S advance or reverse; Holding Q/E rotates the ranks and Leader continuously; releasing the key holds the new angle. Slots 1 and 2 still recall and change stance. Formation movement is 50% faster than its original pace: 105% of the shared normal pace, with wheeling and slot recovery capped at 125% of formation speed. Participating retinue do not collide with each other. Only soldiers whose slots or travel paths meet an obstacle adjust around it; unobstructed soldiers keep their rectangular positions. Narrow passages squeeze the affected ranks, which return to their usual slots when clear. Formed troops hold their ranks and automatically strike hostile units and buildings in reach. A translucent white advance arrow appears ahead of the Leader during rotation, then fades. Their forward 120-degree sector grants +25% damage and +50% defense (incoming damage divided by 1.5); flanks and rear have no bonus.

Press **1 / E**, or click slot 1, to recall the entire retinue, regardless of the selected unit. Warriors immediately abandon their combat targets and regroup without changing the retinue stance or any individual stance. Recalled warriors avoid combat while the Leader is moving. Once regrouped, Hold troops hold their new posts, Defensive troops guard the new rally position, and Aggressive troops resume engagement. New stance commands replace recall.

Completed walls and palisades retain their owner while a completed city building remains within **8 tiles**. After the last such building is gone, each section becomes neutral and can be claimed by the active faction with the most nearby city buildings; equal counts leave it neutral. Settlements, houses, wells, monuments, blacksmiths, and towers count; roads, bridges, walls, and untargetable farms do not. Surviving houses retain defeated factions’ walls. A surviving warcamp tent supports its complete palisade perimeter outside claimed borders; after the last tent is destroyed, unsupported walls become neutral. Raiders cannot claim neutral walls. A gate’s road transfers with its wall. Neutral gates admit everyone; neutral solid walls remain obstacles and do not secure an enclosure.

After defeat, **Respawn** returns a fresh Leader with base stats and zero veterancy to a random vacant, reachable tile within **four tiles** of a surviving settlement. No hostile unit may occupy any of the eight neighboring tiles. Another settlement is tried if one has no safe site. Resources, buildings, and surviving troops retain their progress. Respawn is unavailable when no completed settlement has a safe nearby tile.

## Settlement and combat

Ranged fire from a higher playable elevation tier gains exactly one tile of range: this applies to Leaders, archers, watch towers, Keeps, and ballistae. Flat and uphill shots keep their ordinary range, and obstacles still block shots. Drilled formations put ranged soldiers in the rear ranks. When a Leader routs, every member of that Leader’s retinue immediately retreats as well, abandoning attack orders and formation; escorts cannot rally until their Leader has stopped fleeing. Independent scouts and city guards retain their own orders.

A **Scouts’ Lodge** unlocks after Exploration. Build it with **B then O** for **100 gold**, **45 seconds**, and a **1×1 footprint**, using the native Puny World Medieval thatched cottage. It unlocks observation posts in Fortify and no longer produces automatic explorers. The nearby popup shows the observation-post unlock.

Scouts use the unarmed Puny character and retain seven-tile sight, reduced by forest and extended over lower ground. They use their own exploration goals rather than the Leader’s formation, recall, or stance, and reveal only their faction’s map.

Each completed settlement defines a named city. Its brass-framed dark label sits six screen pixels above the settlement roof, with the city name on the first line and local population / housing, green net food, and gold net income on the second. The label is 60 screen pixels tall, with 18px names and 20px stats in Pixel Operator. Rates use the same **30-game-second** period as the resource HUD; food is potential harvest minus consumption and still needs to be delivered. Houses and farms belong to the nearest friendly settlement, and soldier upkeep belongs to the nearest home city (or current city when unhoused). City names remain with their settlement after confederation. Other factions’ visible city labels show only the name, never population or income. Unseen settlements show no label.

The normal player start has **20 residents, 500 food and 500 gold**, one settlement and a Leader with its population-scaled retinue. Each map has **five opposing civilizations**, each with **two named cities**, **1,500 gold**, and five residents per city. Civilizations use distinct historical names and matching city-name lists. A closed road loop surrounds each settlement. Starting layouts provide housing for their residents and enough accessible farms for their expected initial food demand where terrain permits. Retinue allowance is four base soldiers, plus one per twenty population through 100, one per fifty from 100 through 500, and one per seventy-five thereafter. Fractional population growth carries between economy ticks. At any completed owned settlement within the Leader’s interaction range, **Buy retinue slot** permanently adds one faction-wide slot for **1,000 gold**. Each purchased slot costs **5 gold every thirty game seconds**, occupied or vacant, in addition to ordinary soldier upkeep. Purchases stack across cities and persist through casualties or the loss of the purchasing settlement. The next normal recruitment cycle fills available slots; purchasing does not immediately spawn a soldier or reset the recruitment clock. Fees are charged once per faction, attributed to its capital in city income, and remain payable when the treasury is negative.

A settlement enclosed by completed friendly walls/gates and non-pathable water protects the connected region inside the perimeter. Houses in that region receive **30% faster population growth**, shown by **Pixel glints at Fast speed and 250% intensity**, sweeping southwest to northeast across the enclosed ground, and a **Growth +30%** house label. The effect stops as soon as the enclosure is breached. Fords, completed bridges, missing or unfinished walls, and map-edge gaps break enclosure unless a defensive wall/gate seals the passage. A natural island alone does not grant the bonus. Full protected houses do not pass their bonus to houses outside the region. Automatic housing fills suitable sites inside that settlement’s secure enclosure before considering any outdoor site, including eligible farm conversions. Existing food, borders, block layout, and walking-access rules still apply; manual housing can be placed outside.

A farm is **one planted grid cell**, costing **15 gold** and **3.75 seconds of builder work**. It has no dwelling sprite, residents, or housing capacity. Each field remains traversable and cannot be attacked or damaged. Farms can touch other farms, houses, walls, and other buildings; only overlapping footprints are rejected. A completed basic house accommodates two residents. House levels I–IV provide **2 / 3 / 4 / 5 places**, before the separate +1 farmland-redevelopment and +1 Urban Planning bonuses. Manual and automatic houses share the **65-gold price** and **13⅓-second construction time**. The normal 20-person start supplies eight basic houses plus the settlement’s four beds.

Farm placement limits every **edge-connected block to 16 plots**. **Blocks larger than six plots must have a bounding-box aspect ratio of at most 1.5:1**, measured as the longer side divided by the shorter side; up to six plots may have any shape. Queued farms, fallow plots, orchards, and neighboring factions’ farms count, so construction queues and captures cannot bypass the limit. New placements and relocation destinations use the same rules; removing farmland remains possible.

Automatic farm placement ranks sites by delivery-adjusted yield, including irrigation and actual walking distance. Within the hard block limits, it prefers complete **2×2 and 3×3 squares**, finishing suitable partial parcels before starting another, with **one-cell gaps** between parcels. Other compact shapes remain available where squares do not fit. Gaps are ordinary land, not automatic roads. More productive or more accessible sites may override the layout preference, but cannot exceed the block limits. Starting settlements also prefer these square parcels.

Bring the Leader within **three tiles** of a friendly settlement or blacksmith to see a bright faction-colored silhouette outline and halo. Click the settlement to toggle a compact **Governor permissions:** panel above the roof. Its **Farm** and **Housing** checkboxes independently control that city’s automatic construction and default to enabled. Each settlement has its own development clock and local construction allowances, so one city’s building queue does not delay another; cities still share faction gold. **Import immigrants** costs **100 gold** per click and immediately adds **one resident to the selected city**, without spending food or birth credit. Immigration is disabled when the selected city has no vacant bed; spare housing in another city does not qualify. The game continues while the panel is open; Escape or its red × closes it. Manual construction remains available.

A **well costs 50 gold**, requires **30 population** to order, and brings every completed friendly house within an **eight-tile radius** to **level 2**, including houses completed later. Level 2 houses have three resident spaces and 200 maximum health, and use the original Puny flat-roof house artwork. Overlapping or replacement wells do not stack upgrades, the level-two upgrade remains after its well is destroyed. Level three requires continuing well coverage. Wells use the original Puny covered-well sprite. The build menu grays out unaffordable buildings. **Clear trees** uses a rectangular drag to queue every eligible forest, jungle, or palm tile inside; a settlement peasant walks to each tree and cuts it down before continuing to the next job.

The player faction is **red**. Its cities use a distinct Roman name list beginning **Roma, Ostia, Antium, Capua, and Neapolis**.

A **new settlement costs 100 gold** and takes **90 seconds** of builder work. Use **B, then N** within ten tiles of the Leader. Once complete it creates a named city and projects borders. The settlement provides **four housing places and four food per 30-second economy cycle**, allowing a city to grow from zero residents before its first house or farm. Additional growth needs ordinary housing and agriculture. Residents imported into a city remain assigned there; retinue casualties reduce population in their recruiting city. Its governor can commission farms and housing normally. A fleeing Leader seeks the nearest reachable completed city, skipping unfinished or isolated settlements.

To capture a rival settlement, first destroy that city’s watchtowers and guard posts; houses, libraries, and other civilian buildings may remain. Accepting the capture transfers the city’s civilian buildings, farms, roads, workers, and residents, and restores its settlement at **10% health**. Borders and minimap shading switch immediately, allowing construction in the captured territory. The rival’s other cities retain their ownership. Surviving walls follow their normal nearby-building claims, and enemy soldiers remain hostile.

**Garrison (5)** is available from the start while the Leader is inside a friendly city’s borders. It detaches the current active retinue to defend that city, assigns distinct reachable resting posts, and excludes those defenders from Leader formation, stance, training trips, and distant Leader routs. Defenders engage intruders throughout their city and may pursue an already engaged enemy up to **three tiles outside for five seconds**, then return. They hold still at rest and yield to traffic in narrow gates. **Recall Garrison** stays available when the Leader leaves the city; ordinary Recall also releases them without changing their stance. Losing the defended settlement releases its surviving garrison. The **Mass levy (4)** button becomes **Dismiss levy** while any levied soldiers remain. Drafting costs **15 gold per peasant** immediately; continuing service costs **5 gold per peasant every 30 seconds**, beginning 30 seconds after drafting. Raising a levy must leave a positive treasury, and dismissing it does not refund the draft fee.

Each completed city selects another reachable city of the same faction, preferring the farthest available city. **Each pair shares one caravan**, traveling both ways at **1.3 tiles per second** before terrain modifiers and resting 1.5 seconds after each delivery. Domestic trade uses a complete traversable route quoted before departure: `1.5 × d × (1 + 0.01 × min(d, 50)) × H / (H + 50) × bonuses`. Here `d` is planned route length and `H` is the harmonic mean of the two cities’ population shares. Each city divides its actual residents among its live domestic connections with populated partners; empty connections do not dilute productive routes. An empty endpoint earns nothing. A route with either city at **20 residents or fewer** pays at most **6 gold per trip**, including all bonuses. From 20 to 30 residents, its payout rises rapidly toward the normal formula: at 25 residents it covers 75% of that gap, and at 30 it reaches full value. This maturity check uses each city’s actual residents before splitting demand among routes. Population, bonuses and path value are fixed for that leg; reroutes and wandering cannot enlarge the payment. Fractional gold is credited exactly, while the UI rounds the amount. The distance premium stops at **+50%**, and population returns diminish toward a fixed multiplier of one. On established land roads, ordinary steady trade approaches **131.625 gold per 30 seconds per caravan** before bonuses as distance and population become very large. Merchants establish roads on eligible cells, relocating farms for free and retaining irrigation, without paving water, stairs, solid buildings or closed walls. Blocked or disconnected journeys pay nothing. Killing a merchant awards **200 gold**; replacement takes at least **four game minutes**, including after route closure or ownership changes. Village agreements use a separate distance formula and are not population-scaled.

**River trade** becomes available with Sailing at no additional cost. Eligible river partners take priority over farther land-only cities. City pairs automatically receive small native shore trading posts and use one existing caravan, which walks to the bank, sails at the merchant’s **1.3-tile base speed**, and walks into the destination. Both landing banks and their adjacent river cells must lie inside the respective cities’ local borders, with reachable walking access. The connected river leg must be longer than the walking leg at **each** end; oceans, lakes, impassable fords, bridge decks and cliff edges cannot substitute for river navigation. River distance counts **2.5×** land distance in the fixed departure quote, with the same population, maturity, distance-premium and specialty rules. An already travelling land caravan completes its outgoing and return quotes before changing partners at its origin; if another caravan already serves the river pair, the returning land caravan retires there without a bounty or casualty delay. A pair never receives a second caravan. Obstructed boats wait or replan without increasing the quote. The merchant remains damageable, retains its **200-gold bounty**, and waits **240 game seconds** for replacement after death.

Wild **boars live in herds of 1–8**. Undisturbed herds graze in place for **22–38 seconds**, occasionally taking a slow forage walk of at most **five seconds**. They spend over 80% of their time stationary and only run when humans approach, settling again after the threat passes. Every spawn cell must be outside current player sight, away from humans and settlements; after losses, at most one herd returns every **3–5 minutes**. Five food covers one meal cycle for the initial five-person village; its normal growth is about 0.74 births per 30 seconds, so a 10% increase needs only 0.30 extra food for births. Five is the smallest useful positive reward rounded to five. Since stored food does not directly determine the growth rate, the explicit timed multiplier supplies the exact increase. Only newly earned growth is boosted; partial economy ticks are time-weighted. Food availability and vacant housing still constrain births.

**Marsh birds live in flocks within coastal wetlands.** They forage among the reeds and cross the marsh’s water channels, staying inside their wetland habitat. Hunting a bird immediately awards **20 food**, with a matching food popup. A **library costs 200 gold**, has **750 health**, occupies **2×2 tiles**, and takes **90 seconds** of builder work (**B, then I**). Science progresses automatically while at least one completed library survives. Paid income enters a Science reserve and releases over **30 game seconds**, spreading tax and trade receipts across continuous progress. At 1× speed even a huge payout advances by at most **one percentage point per 0.2 seconds**. No progress is created without paid Science. The cheapest technology whose prerequisites are complete is chosen with a stable tie order. **Prioritize** moves any unfinished technology to the front of a shared faction queue; its unmet prerequisites are automatically researched first. Changing priorities preserves earlier progress and redirects the remaining reserve. Extra Science carries into the next project. Multiple libraries do not multiply progress. Zero allocation stops new funding but lets already-paid work finish; losing every library pauses the reserve. A fully funded tree takes no more gold, and scholar discounts refund any excess reserve without generating new income or Science. Controls remain at the library; the top toolbar and a thin bar above friendly libraries show progress, while enemy progress stays hidden.

The tree spans **six stages**, beginning with **Fortification, Irrigation and Exploration**. Allied villages defend against raiders and can be fought in return. Destroying every tent stops that camp’s waves; after **120 game seconds**, a replacement is attempted in the current fog of war. Replacement waits and retries if no hidden site is available. Surviving raiders keep fighting.

The first **large camp wave** begins after one minute. A separate opening three-member party follows a bounded walking route immediately, targeting first contact within thirty seconds. Additional **1–5-member skirmishes depart every 18–24 game seconds per full faction**, reusing local bands or camp reserves first. Small attacks and camp defenders do not consume the eight large-wave slots, but share all unit-population limits. Staggered wave intervals decrease linearly from **26 to 14 seconds over the first 15 game minutes**, while existing wave-size growth continues up to **60 raiders per wave**. Raider weapon and armor multipliers rise linearly from **1.0× to 1.6×** over the same period, independent of blacksmith upgrades and remaining capped thereafter. Living raiders and new recruits receive the same time-based multiplier without compounding it. Each party remains limited to **30 including its leader**, with **90 living raiders per camp**, **240 globally**, and **eight active large waves**. Returning, wandering, garrisoned and unassigned raiders count toward saturation; blocked waves do not accumulate a spawning backlog. A bouncing red exclamation mark warns of a wave during its final ten seconds, subject to fog visibility. Full factions continue commanding their armies through their Leaders.

Neutral villages are scattered at a target of **1.5 per full faction**, subject to suitable space. Each begins with **three white tents around an open clearing** and **2–5 white-clad villagers**, with no Leader or walls. Visible peaceful village buildings have silhouette highlights to show they are interactive, including buildings beyond the Leader’s local activity range. Hidden or remembered buildings do not gain highlights through fog. Left- or right-click any visible village building or villager to open the same diplomacy popup used to declare war, with **Trade**, **Alliance**, and **Declare War** choices. These remote agreements require a living Leader, not a separate village visit. **Trade costs 250 gold** and opens one caravan connection. Each completed journey pays both partners `3 × round(0.2 × base × bonuses)` gold, where `base = round(d × (1 + 0.01 × floor(d)))` and `d` is distance traveled. Village trade has no city-population scaling. **Alliance starts at 3,000 gold**, reduced by one gold for every ten gold that village has earned from completed trade journeys with you, to a **500-gold minimum**. The discount uses lifetime revenue, so village spending does not erase it; trade with other factions does not discount your agreement. Alliance remains a separate purchase from trade. Allies defend against your enemies near their camp, share **20-tile sight**, and show a floating handshake above their central clearing. Declare War makes the village hostile and ends its agreements.

Each tribe’s identity supplies one faction-wide alliance bonus: **Growers +10% harvest yield; Merchants +15% trade income; Builders +15% construction and repair speed; Scholars +15% Science efficiency (science costs ÷ 1.15)**. Duplicate identities do not stack. Destroying the main tent removes its bonus, shared sight and trade connection. Each completed village caravan journey pays both trading partners. Villages spend their treasury on **clusters of one to three houses**, improved housing up to level three, guards, and defenses. Development is limited to **ten houses, twelve guards, two guard posts and four wooden watchtowers**. Houses cost 40 gold each, housing upgrades cost 50/100, guards cost 40, guard posts 120, and towers 100; construction and recruitment advance on ninety-second timers. Prosperous rival factions can establish one reachable village route after reserving 1,000 gold for their cities and paying the usual 250-gold agreement fee. Surviving villages replace at most one civilian per ninety seconds, without farms or food consumption, up to twenty civilians. They remain unwalled and without a faction Leader. Villages do not count toward civilization victory.

**Automatically rebuild defenses** is enabled by default in Settings. Combat-destroyed walls, palisades, and towers are reordered at their original locations with their normal gold cost and builder work, even when the Leader is away. Rebuilding waits for resources, preserves existing gates, and stops claiming locations taken by another faction. Turning the setting off clears pending rebuild requests; construction already ordered continues. Deliberately demolished structures are not rebuilt.

Marauder camp lanes and gate tracks use narrow dirt-road artwork, including in already active camps.

Raider bands also receive specialist motivations. **Harass** bands (2–10) prey on civilians and undefended buildings and retreat on spotting troops. **Theft** bands (1–10) use an open settlement route, steal the greater of 200 gold or half the treasury (capped to available gold), and run home. Killing the carrier transfers the stolen amount to the killer in addition to the usual bounty; reaching a surviving camp house permanently removes it. Thieves fight blockers or when they have a three-to-one local advantage. Infrequent **sieges** muster outside defensive range, gain reinforcements, then launch a large assault; stronger defenders increase the target size. All existing camp, group and global population caps still apply. Raider archers progressively rise to a **20% share** by twelve minutes, one-third below the previous mix; commanders remain melee leaders. Camp-defense parties retain two-thirds of their former archer assignments on average.

Rival cities reconsider development every **six seconds**, using the same gold costs, builders, construction times, farm spoilage, and housing rules as the player. They fund food supply early, prioritize full housing when existing food and stores can support growth, reserve money for productive buildings before perimeter walls, and buy wells when they efficiently upgrade several homes. Monuments are considered when borders prevent further housing. Spare-bed construction remains at ten percent of the normal rate.

Faction armies use their Leader’s shared campaign orders, never independent raider archetypes or detached raid waves. They assemble escorts before attacking a reachable hostile city, wait for stragglers, defend their homes, and retreat when endangered; only wilderness raiders retain their distinct behaviors. Rival Leaders scout with escorts and hunt observed, reachable raider groups for their actual **6-gold warrior / 8-gold archer / 50-gold commander bounties**. They assess health, morale, numbers, veterancy, and nearby enemy towers; keep at least one soldier and roughly a quarter of the available army at home; regroup stragglers; and prioritize home alarms. Leaders pursue fleeing quarry while their javelins recharge, without receiving speed or combat bonuses. Searches use last-seen positions briefly, hunts stay within 26 tiles of the settlement and last at most 38 seconds, and wounded or overextended parties return inside their borders to heal. Active hunts and emergencies take precedence over new sorties against the player.

Marauders are either assigned an attack against a reachable faction or sheltered inside a surviving camp tent, where they perform no movement or pathfinding. Initial surplus bands begin as sleeping camp reserves. Returning bands enter tents; idle active bands receive an attack or return order instead of wandering. Damage to a marauder rallies allies and sleeping reserves within **14 tiles**, sharing the attacker’s last known position for **18 seconds**. Retreating commanders keep their refuge orders. Marauders outside every human player’s current sight run their AI, movement and pack planning once every **five simulation steps**, staggered by ID. Skipped time accumulates for movement and morale; existing-target attacks, cooldowns, projectiles and healing retain their normal cadence. They return to full updates as soon as a player can see them.

Every marauder source deals **15% less damage**, applied once after armor to melee and projectile hits. Raid counts, timing and defensive strength retain their existing values.

Each raiding party has at most **30 total members, including exactly one leader when it spawns**. Increasing late-game raids add more parties rather than exceeding this limit.

Each marauder band has a **melee leader** with **20% more health** and **20% more speed** than the prior leader balance: **3.525 tiles per second** (1.5× ordinary marauder speed), **78 health** for roaming leaders, and 1.2× the current wave’s health for camp leaders. They remain worth **50 gold** when killed. Leaders flee when injured or outnumbered. Returning bands physically enter surviving camp tents and become inactive, with at most thirty sleeping reserves per camp. Later raids reuse those soldiers. Orphaned and stalled bands are reassigned to reachable camps or attack objectives. Each attacked camp structure calls **2–5 defenders**, including warriors and archers, with a **30-second cooldown per building**; repeat hits cannot trigger a new squad every frame. Defender recruitment shares the existing population caps.

**Menu → Options → Bandit intensity** adjusts single-player raid pressure from **0% to 300%**, in 25% steps. **100%** retains normal pacing and population limits; **200%** doubles spawn frequency and the global/per-camp limits (480/180), while individual parties still contain at most thirty soldiers. This changes numbers and frequency, not unit strength. **0%** stops new raiders without deleting existing enemies. Changes apply to the current game, persist in saves, and carry into new single-player worlds. Multiplayer matches retain their server-controlled setting.

A rout can spread through hostile armies: visible routing allies within five tiles drain up to **18 morale per second** during nearby fighting, in proportion to routing soldiers versus those still standing. A nearby commander who is still standing halves this pressure. Stable formations can hold; depleted lines can collapse in a chain reaction. Routed marauders remain routed for **at least 15 game seconds**, including while returning to or sheltering in a camp.

Units earn **Regular**, **Veteran**, **Hardened**, and **Elite** status at **10**, **100**, **250**, and **500** enemy kills. Regular, Veteran, and Hardened use one, two, or three compact gold chevrons close to the unit; Elite uses the Bright core gold thunderbolt. The first three ranks add **3/7/12 base attack** and **2/5/8 armor** respectively, with the same additions for warriors and archers. Elite instead deals **2.3× damage** and takes **15% damage**, relative to the same untrained unit with the same equipment, after normal armor and the global damage reduction. These multipliers replace the additive rank bonuses and apply to melee, arrows, javelins, and tower hits. Scouts remain noncombatants. The selection panel shows rank and Elite resistance. Retinue kills belong to three-person squads, surviving squads pass their rank to replacements, and only a complete squad loss erases it. Guard-post experience belongs to the post; Leaders track their own kills. Kill counts persist in saved games; bonuses are derived rather than repeatedly added to base stats.

Farm fields clear trees and swamp overlays only within their four reserved tiles. Crops follow the rendered shoreline mask and never paint over water. Housing destruction triggers rehousing when capacity exists; otherwise population is lost. Warrior deaths also cost population.

Units regenerate **2.4 health per second after 15 seconds without taking damage**, up to their own maximum health, **only inside their own faction’s borders**. This includes peasants and units of other full factions. **Marauder commanders** are the exception: they regenerate anywhere after fifteen seconds without either attacking or taking damage. Ordinary raiders and all civilization units retain the border requirement. Settlements, completed houses, and monuments project tile territory, outlined with alternating white and faction-color dashes and gently rounded corners. Where two factions meet, a single shared line alternates white, the first faction’s color, white, and the second faction’s color. The line is 25% thinner and uses 75% opacity. **Population does not affect border size.** Settlements and houses retain their 30% border buffer, giving base radii of 6.5 and 3.25 tiles. The first starting settlement of each civilization receives an additional **three-tile radius**; later settlements retain the ordinary starting radius. Completed monuments claim a two-tile radius immediately, without waiting for a culture threshold. Each culture expansion adds 1.3 tiles to every source’s radius. Living full factions and neutral villages project borders; raider camps and unowned structures do not. All borders are hidden in unexplored heavy fog, including on the minimap, and remain visible in explored light fog. Village borders are **solid white**, begin at **three tiles**, and grow with residents and completed houses/defenses to at most **six tiles from the village center**. Their land remains reserved against outside culture and every kind of foreign construction, including farms, roads and settlement road rings. Village borders grant no player sight by themselves; alliance vision continues to work normally. Faction and village borders appear only on discovered tiles, both in the world and on the minimap. Discovered boundaries remain visible through light fog. This does not expose hidden terrain, buildings, units or construction. The strongest relative-distance claim wins overlapping land. Claims extend across rivers, with no bank outlines inside a continuous claim; lakes, coasts, and ocean stay unclaimed. For ordinary units, dealing damage or being targeted does not reset the delay; an actual melee hit or projectile impact does. Marauder commanders must also stop attacking to recover. Buildings still require a repair peasant.

All unit and tower hits deal **30% less damage**, applied after armor, including minimum hits. Combat includes pursuit, loot, morale, and routing. A hit immediately drains a peasant’s morale and makes them abandon their current job and flee toward safety. They retain carried food and resume work after danger passes. Within eight tiles, friendly deaths over the last **20 seconds** trigger casualty collapse at half the local living-plus-fallen cohort, rounded up, with a **minimum of six**. Friendly losses must also be at least **five times nearby enemy losses in that same window**. The shock lasts **four seconds** and drains morale at 50 per second while that ratio holds. A non-leader rout caused by it recovers at the four-second deadline, restoring at least 50 morale and the previous order. Consumed casualties cannot immediately restart the episode. Other ordinary routs keep their normal recovery rules. A marauder commander’s death immediately removes **40 morale** from living marauder soldiers within **six tiles**, including neighboring bands. The shock ignores player visibility and triggers once per death; units reduced to zero rout immediately. Sheltered reserves and other factions are unaffected.

A routed **Leader immediately retreats to a reachable edge of its settlement**, ahead of yielding or combat orders. It stops routing on arrival and recovers at least 50 morale; the four-second shock timeout alone does not interrupt that retreat. Defeated factions do not recover this way.

Units prioritize reachable towers that recently fired at them or nearby allies, then nearby military units, civilians, other buildings, and finally walls. They use friendly gates and existing breaches to reach city targets. A sealed city remains the objective while attackers break a blocking wall section, then enter through the opening instead of clearing the perimeter. Temporary crowds at an entrance do not trigger wall demolition. Proximity and available melee contact cells guide targeting. Melee cannot strike through walls or water. Javelins pass over walls but not other solid buildings. Explicit attack orders remain in effect. An idle player leader throws javelins within four tiles without pursuing; movement takes precedence. Leaders and retinue warriors have 100 health, peasants 50, and each raider starts at its own full maximum health. Five-section health bars use those actual maxima. Puny Characters supply eight-direction figures and animations, with faction-colored chest cloth and matching HUD portraits.

Right-click any visible peaceful faction’s structure to open a **Declare War** popup, without visiting its settlement. Cancel or Escape leaves relations and troop orders unchanged; selecting Declare War confirms it. Right-clicking an already hostile structure still issues an attack order.

Confederation is chance-based and affected by cultural affinity. Wartime offers have half the normal chance. Rejected offers may provoke war. Victory comes when every rival has joined or been defeated. The victory panel offers **Keep playing** alongside **New world**; Escape or the close button also resumes the same world. That victory is acknowledged once, including after a save/reload, while losing the Leader or all owned settlements can still cause defeat. Both camp waves and small marauder raids continue after resuming. Siege groups blocked on their approach return after thirty seconds without movement or combat, freeing their wave slot; groups that have reached their rally point retain the ninety-second muster deadline.

Procedural worlds are **194×153 tiles**, approximately half the previous tile area. On first launch, **New game**, or **N**, choose **Continents**, **Pangaea**, **Fractal**, or the larger geographic **Mediterreanean** world and a seed. Fractal remains the default based on gallery sample #20, with the newer compact-lake and river rules. Continents varies its landmass proportions and curved straits; Pangaea varies overlapping peninsulas, bays and necks; Fractal varies branching mainland lobes and offshore island chains. Procedural land geometry uses rotated Natural Earth patches with minimum-error overlap cuts. All six civilizations and eleven towns share reachable land.

Production worlds first construct a small set of related geological provinces with connected ridge and valley curves, basin shapes, rock resistance and continuous elevation. A bounded erosion approximation shapes the field before it is converted to native ledges. Contour-based plateaus, arbitrary-angle mountain belts, rift shoulders and rounded uplands replace a uniform mixture of small relief stamps. Geological lake basins are placed before cliffs; other freshwater lakes fill scattered suitable inland sites. All lakes are compact, with measured aspect ratios at or below two to one; elongated faults and valleys contain compact water pockets instead of long snaky lakes. The river network follows downhill catchments and incises completed landforms, rebuilding native ledge banks. Main rivers are **2–4 map tiles wide**; small tributaries remain one tile wide. Major catchments route around lakes. A lake can receive a modest feeder and drains through one narrow outlet, with no large-river endpoint on its shore. The retained drainage graph conserves rainfall runoff and reaches the ocean. Clear stair landings preserve navigation. Small headwater streams can descend surviving cliff shelves through the original animated Puny waterfalls, with downstream connections to existing rivers, lakes or sea. Drops use the authored south-facing ledge geometry and can form cascades across successive shelves; other cliff crossings retain canyon carving. Waterfalls block walking, boats, fishing and bridge placement. Literal sea cliffs are not generated. The geological model is a controlled game-scale approximation, not a full tectonic, glacial or sediment-transport simulation.

Ground between cliff shelves is flat. Native ledges and stair crossings retain their existing movement, sight, and ranged-combat rules.

Climate uses deterministic prevailing winds, moisture from oceans and lakes, uphill rainfall, rain shadows, coastal temperature moderation and elevation cooling. **Temperate country, taiga, tundra, rainforest, savanna, steppe, desert, wetland and alpine country** reuse existing grass, sand and tree artwork. The temperature and rainfall values are dimensionless gameplay fields. Forests retain multiple regional patch sizes, with individual trees interspersed with open ground throughout each woodland. No 2×2 area is completely wooded, and every tree adjoins traversable open ground; cold uplands have sparser vegetation. Wetlands move at 70% speed, alpine ground at 80%, and tundra at 90%; forests retain their 50% speed when slower. Roads and bridges retain their normal overrides.

Soil fertility follows freshwater, slope, temperature, moisture and regional soil variation. It multiplies base farm harvests by **55–145%**, with the existing desert penalty, irrigation and orchard bonuses applied separately. Governors evaluate these same actual harvests when choosing farms. Starting sites favor viable ground and freshwater access, preserve local ground types, and assign rival second cities using overland route distance. Settlement woodland clearings remain in place.

Regional **clay, iron, stone, salt and fish** deposits follow geological patches and appropriate habitats. Three accessible deposit cells within eight tiles establish a city's trade specialty; fisheries and quarries can be worked from adjoining ground. Completed bridges can unlock deposits across water. Each resource type present at only one end of a trade route adds **5%** to delivered gold, up to **25%**. No additional construction currency is introduced. Press **Y** to see resource markers and inspect biome, soil, terrain speed, harvests and discoveries; selecting your settlement lists its specialties and route bonuses. Rival inspection does not reveal hidden trade partners.

Boar herds prefer moist, productive ground with nearby cover and freshwater. Habitat quality determines population capacity, home selection and foraging destinations; dry deserts, beaches and cold exposed uplands cannot support ordinary herds. Up to four rare named caches per production world identify existing ancient groves, high passes, lake shores, river junctions or coastal headlands. They reuse the treasure chest artwork and award 250–325 gold; ordinary wilderness chests still award 200.

New worlds place connected sandy beaches along **about 43% of the ocean-facing coastline**, extending one to three tiles inland. Sparse native Medieval World palms appear only on warm-latitude beach tiles and remain at least three tiles apart. Palms are scenery rather than dense forest: they do not reduce movement or sight, and Clear Trees or construction can remove them. Inland lakes and riverbanks keep their existing terrain rules. Ocean water uses the original Puny turquoise shelf, cyan middle water, and deep blue tiles, with the deep band beginning seven tiles from land. Sandy shores have a three-tile turquoise shelf; grassy banks retain their native cyan shore. The minimap reflects those water bands. Sand, foam, depth transitions, and all four wave frames come directly from the unchanged Puny World sheet.

Faction capitals use farthest-point placement and constrained relaxation across viable land to avoid crowded clusters; rival second cities favor their own capital. Six civilizations begin on mutually reachable land: the player starts with one city and each rival with two. Offshore landmasses still have normal terrain features; there is no naval travel. Some rivers have spaced ford crossings. Balance remains at prototype stage.

The game saves to this browser every fifteen seconds and when the tab becomes hidden. Save files share repeated terrain property names without rounding values, keeping a new production world below the browser storage quota; victory continuation is retained on reload. Reloading restores fields, harvests, deliveries, construction and repair assignments, recent traffic, unit damage timers, roaming packs, and raider camp schedules. Destroyed camps stay destroyed. Older saves convert stone defenses to wooden ones, discard the retired material resource, clear obsolete terrain overlays, and initialize missing camps and independent packs once. Newly added roaming packs stay outside current player sight. Food and gold balances remain intact. **New game** or **N** opens the world chooser; only **Generate** replaces the saved world with the selected shape, seed, and current generation rules. No external fonts or runtime services are required.

Ground, shorelines, water, and roads use the Puny World atlas. Wild trees use only the Medieval World pack, grouped into tropical, temperate, boreal, and alpine families by latitude and local climate. Orchards use the original fruit trees from Puny World. Fields progress from short light-green seedlings to taller green crops to the original PixelHole golden wheat, with its wooden crate marking waiting harvests. Farm plots have no decorative fencing. Connected terrain tiles retain the pack's pixel edges and palette. Overlays stay opaque and render below units. Combat shows brief blood effects but no floating damage numbers. Gold earned from defeating enemies appears briefly as **+6** for warriors, **+8** for archers, or **+50** for a marauder leader, without the word “gold”, at a visible death location and never reveals deaths through fog.

New maps scatter reachable Puny treasure chests through unexplored wilderness, away from settlements and raider camps. A living player Leader collects a chest by entering its tile, once: ordinary chests award **200 gold**, while named geographic discoveries award **250–325 gold**. Chests never block movement or reveal unexplored terrain.

The map begins unexplored. **Every tile owned by the player is always fully observed**, including forest and claimed river tiles, with no fog edge shading inside the claim. Outside that territory, land cover along each viewing ray consumes sight distance: forests and jungle reduce range by **70%** in continuous woodland, while open terrain retains normal range. Intervening stands also shorten sight, and clearing trees immediately restores visibility. Player combatants share their faction’s current sight; enemy combatants use their own sight, and hidden units cannot be newly targeted or tracked. Player units and completed buildings reveal their surroundings through tile-based fog with softly feathered, rounded edges; roads, bridges, construction sites, and collectors inside settlements do not provide sight. Unexplored terrain is black, while explored areas outside current sight are shaded. Visual fog lingers briefly after sight is lost, then gradually returns; newly revealed areas fade clear. Brief changes at the edge of sight no longer make the fog flash on and off. This visual smoothing does not reveal hidden enemy activity outside current sight. Unseen unit movement, health, interactions, and new field harvest crates are hidden. Completed enemy buildings and farm fields retain their last scouted appearance and crop state until their locations are seen again. Enemy construction sites, pending fields, and construction indicators remain hidden even in direct sight. Player tree-clearing orders remain visible above fog without revealing the terrain underneath. The minimap uses the same visibility rules, and exploration persists in saves. Older saves begin revealing from the player's current positions.

The game uses Shade's CC0 Puny World, Puny Dungeon, Puny Characters, and Miniworld packs. Housing is the original tent at level 1, flat-roof house at level 2, gabled house at level 3, and gabled chimney house at level 4. The original well is static. Settlement, tower, and gate artwork uses native atlas pieces; all building ownership colors follow the actual faction color. Native pixel scale and the existing terrain rules are retained. Faction chest cloth and supplied HUD references are documented additions. See [ASSETS.md](ASSETS.md) and [terrain provenance](src/world/data/PROVENANCE.md) for sources and licenses.

### Decision update rates

Expensive quiet-unit sensing, stationary rally layouts, idle worker job selection, and background builder/farm/courier/trade maintenance use staggered **3 Hz** decisions. Active combat sensing runs at **8 Hz**. Entity IDs spread deadlines across frames without consuming gameplay randomness. Commands, damage, invalid targets, and relevant world or workforce changes wake decisions immediately; siege approach/contact assignment stays fast where units compete for a breach.

Farming walking-distance maps batch **20 ordinary road/farm/irrigation edits** before refreshing. A partial batch publishes within **6.67 game seconds** of its first observed edit. Bridges, gates, solid buildings, settlement changes, diplomacy, and otherwise unexplained terrain revisions refresh immediately. Courier home assignments follow the same published map revision, including after a timeout with no further construction. Movement and collision checks continue using current navigation geometry. Farming cost estimates can therefore lag routine paving briefly, while harvests, spoilage, pickups, and deliveries keep their existing timing.

The October 31 controlled benchmark measured **20× fewer farm-map rebuilds** (440 → 22 across 40 road edits) and **18.6× faster summary calls** on average (45.20 → 2.43 ms), across three alternating trials with 150 residents and 11 settlements. Run it with `node --expose-gc scripts/benchmark-farm-refresh.mjs --baseline=<pre-change-source> --out=<results>`. A separate 25-second browser replay pair measured **47.2 → 52.7 FPS**, average frame work **17.97 → 14.81 ms**, and p95 **60 → 36 ms**. This single pair has different simulation trajectories; it is not a guaranteed FPS gain. Individual refresh frames and other large spikes remain. See [the farming navigation benchmark record](benchmarks/farm-navigation-refresh-2026-10-31.json).

Movement, collision/yielding, attack cooldowns and execution, projectiles, morale integration, worker arrival/pickup/delivery, and construction still advance every simulation step; research advances only when gold income arrives. Existing slower strategy/planning clocks and exact harvest/spoilage/economy deadlines retain their own schedules. All clocks use game time, so pause and speed controls work normally. Quiet autonomous reactions can take up to one third of a game second; combat sensing can take up to one eighth, plus the next simulation step. ### Runtime libraries

The entity store owns unit/building membership, ID lookups, and bitECS role queries. Systems add, retain, replace, and change roles explicitly; readonly arrays on GameState are serializable views of the same objects. Queries do not reconcile or rescan arrays. Spatial buckets and query storage survive between updates. Loading a state creates runtime indexes once; these indexes are not serialized.

tinyqueue schedules harvest/spoilage, city-planning, and tree-stump deadlines on simulation time. Queues rebuild after a world replacement or rewind, reject cancelled/replaced events, and preserve the previous city spending order. Pause and simulation speed retain their existing behavior.

The world renderer composes native PixiJS sprites and Graphics directly, preserving painter order, pixel-art filtering, terrain, fog, and crop occlusion. Figures use sprite-sheet texture frames; existing Canvas art helpers bake shared bounded textures for buildings, text, and complex clips. ### WebAssembly navigation

Production builds compile the weighted A* loop in `assembly/navigation.ts` into `public/wasm/navigation.wasm`. The browser loads it automatically; terrain and heap storage stay in reusable native memory. Queries against the live spatial index use the existing JavaScript pathfinder, because serializing all units for each local probe costs more than checking the few visited cells. Searches with reusable terrain and snapshot blockers run in WebAssembly. Unavailable WebAssembly or native allocation failure also falls back to JavaScript. This is a compiled hot loop, not a worker or a claim of multicore execution.

Build the kernel with `npm run build:wasm` (also part of `npm run build`). `tests/navigation-wasm.test.ts` checks exact paths, tie ordering, partial goals, cardinal fallback, terrain invalidation, and rectangular buildings against JavaScript. `scripts/benchmark-navigation-wasm.mjs` compares the two backends over captured route requests. `scripts/check-native-simulation.mjs` compares complete state digests after a fixed-step settlement replay; `scripts/profile-live-state.mjs --navigation=js|wasm` measures full browser frames. Browser replay scripts accept `--state`, `--url`, and `--out`.

The October 29 isolated comparison started from the captured 1,036-unit, 1,706-building settlement on a 224×176 map. The median batch of 250 real route requests fell from **142.70 to 56.85 ms (2.51× faster)**. In 30-second browser replays at 2× speed, full-frame mean fell from **20.51 to 18.51 ms**, p95 from **32.2 to 26.7 ms**, and p99 from **50.9 to 37.3 ms**. That is **9.7% less mean frame work**, rather than a 2.51× speedup of the whole game. The native replay executed 22,032 compiled searches; the control executed zero. A separate 300-update / 60-game-second check produced byte-identical serialized state with 21,106 native searches, including identical unit positions, health, buildings, and RNG state. Some frame outliers remain above 40 ms. These comparisons isolate navigation from the other gameplay changes on this branch. See [the benchmark record](benchmarks/navigation-wasm-2026-10-29.json).

A final 20-second replay of the **complete feature branch**, including the city, civilian, guard, and raid changes, averaged **20.01 ms/frame** with **28.2 ms p95** at 2× speed (1,054 units, 1,712 buildings). It executed 20,943 native searches without browser errors. Nineteen of 962 frames exceeded 40 ms; p99 was 51.8 ms. ## Terrain, foliage and conquest

Cultural reach uses the same per-tile travel cost as line of sight: forest and jungle consume 3⅓ times as much reach as open ground. Culture still expands the reach budget at the existing thresholds, but woodland produces irregular, connected city borders instead of identical circles. Monument initial reach is two tiles, and each monument supplies ⅔ culture per second. Road junctions may be two tiles apart.

Trees and orchard canopies share the scene depth order with units and buildings. A unit behind the trunk is covered by foliage; a unit whose feet pass the trunk toward the near side draws in front. Wheat hides feet and lower legs while leaving the upper body visible. Ripe orchards use native fruit-bearing trees with terrain visible beneath them. Felled trees leave matching native stumps for thirty simulation seconds; stumps do not obstruct movement.

When the player destroys a rival settlement, simulation waits for a **Capture city / Leave destroyed** decision. Capturing immediately places a completed player settlement on the same footprint, retaining the city name and starting at **10% health**. Declining or closing the popup leaves it destroyed. The last opponent’s defeat waits for this decision before showing victory.

Each ledge plateau and its two-tile land apron use a uniform grassland or desert terrain. Overlapping aprons share a terrain choice, while waterways are preserved.

Civilian workers and merchants flee from nearby visible hostile units only when the enemy has a traversable route to them. Enemy gate permissions, water, ridges and completed walls all count: a sealed town remains calm, while a breach immediately refreshes threat sensing. An actual hit still causes immediate panic.

World creation offers an **Advanced start** checkbox for single player: **250 residents**, enough housing, **twelve retinue soldiers**, and a completed example of every buildable structure. Charges recharge for **eight seconds** and require a new run-up. Waiting, sharp turns, blocked movement, flight, or teleporting cannot charge the attack. Normal damage handling preserves armor, squad kills, and population losses.


Each cardinally connected same-owner crop parcel has **one collector**, subject to available residents after construction staffing. Adjacent orchards and wheat are separate jobs. Merging parcels releases surplus collectors and preserves their cargo; splitting or capturing parcels rebuilds assignments without duplicate workers. A parcel spanning two cities still has one worker. Harvest departures remain coordinated independently by city, spread across a **two-second window** with at most **0.3 seconds** between scheduled starts. Collectors reserve and sweep all reachable grain in their parcel. Only active collection inside that parcel pauses ground-food and cargo spoilage; waiting, fleeing, blocked movement after two seconds, travel outside the parcel and the return trip do not. Urgent harvests bypass dispatch delay; losses, flight, stalled routes, ownership changes, and completed trips release reservations. In the controlled four-parcel, 64-plot harvest, **four collectors make four trips**, carry up to **16 pickups** each and deliver all 64 food, with at most one departure per simulation tick.
