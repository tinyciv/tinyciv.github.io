Combat fixtures use 12 attackers against 8 stationary defenders, with high health to keep geometry comparable throughout the 24-second run. Crossing traffic sends 24 friendly units to distinct destinations. Both versions use the same positions, terrain, speeds, combat stats, random seed and 0.1-second steps. This laboratory never accesses the game’s saved world.

Methods, limits and sources
  • Attackers reserve separate contact cells and choose another military target when the closest one has no free contact position. Soldiers already fighting retain their place while reinforcements route around them.
  • Weighted A* adds local occupancy and short planned-route congestion costs. Stable yielding gives leaders priority and allows idle units to step aside; completed routes no longer make stationary units falsely report that they are travelling.
  • These are destination reservations and local congestion heuristics, inspired by David Silver’s Cooperative Pathfinding (2005) and the planning/steering separation in Craig Reynolds’ Steering Behaviors (1999). They are not a full space-time CA* or ORCA implementation, and do not guarantee optimal multi-agent paths.
  • Idle unit-seconds counts stationary time beyond a one-second grace period, excluding combat and completed destinations. Engagement means the unit dealt or received damage within the last 1.1 seconds. Overlaps count samples with two living units in one grid cell. Congestion in a physically full passage still requires waiting.
