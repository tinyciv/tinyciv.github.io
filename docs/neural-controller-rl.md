# Reinforcement learning for the TinyCiv controller

Train a small faction-level policy to decide when to concentrate the army, keep a reserve, retreat, or resume fighting. Use the production headless simulation to collect experience and the existing mirrored match harness to evaluate it. The first milestone is a controller that beats the strongest checked-in field policy on unseen generated terrain and fits within a measured browser CPU budget. Siege and empire management each need additional decisions, training starts, and evidence before they can be included in that claim.

## What video-game RL research suggests

| System | Result and implication for TinyCiv |
| --- | --- |
| Atari DQN and NetHack Learning Environment | DQN demonstrated learning from pixels across 49 Atari games. NetHack showed how a fast, procedurally generated game with compact observations can support long-horizon RL research. TinyCiv already has the semantic information needed by a faction planner, so structured observations save both simulation and learning work. [Atari DQN](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14236), [NetHack Learning Environment](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/hash/569ff987c643b4bedf504efda8f786c2-Abstract.html) |
| StarCraft II Learning Environment and AlphaStar | SC2LE provides focused mini-games alongside the full game. AlphaStar used structured unit observations, imitation from human replays, and a league of strategies and counterstrategies. Use focused TinyCiv situations to debug and learn useful tactics, then test on full maps and train against opponents that force different responses. [SC2LE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04782), [AlphaStar paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1724-z), [DeepMind league details](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphastar-grandmaster-level-in-starcraft-ii-using-multi-agent-reinforcement-learning/) |
| Gym-μRTS and RAISocketAI | Gym-μRTS reported about 60 hours on one GPU for a strong single-map bot. The later competition winner used transfer to specific maps and about 70 GPU-days across its models. Its follow-up showed that cloning scripted play and fine-tuning with win/loss PPO can work without handcrafted shaping. This is the closest precedent for TinyCiv’s masked actions and existing scripted opponents. The two compute figures describe different experiments. [Gym-μRTS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.13807), [RAISocketAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v2) |
| OpenAI Five | Dota 2 showed that PPO and self-play can handle long games and imperfect information with a large simulation budget. The team acted every fourth game frame, used a recurrent network, and trained at a scale of roughly two million frames per two seconds. For TinyCiv, the transferable ideas are separating decision cadence from engine cadence and saving reproducible policy checkpoints. [OpenAI Five](https://cdn.openai.com/dota-2.pdf) |
| Procgen and CoinRun | Training on a limited set of procedural levels can produce strong training scores and weak generalization. Split by original generator seed before creating snapshots, and hold out geography families as a separate test of transfer. [Procgen](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/cobbe20a.html), [CoinRun](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/cobbe19a.html) |
| DreamerV3 and MuZero | DreamerV3 learned Minecraft diamond collection from sparse rewards, reaching the milestone within 100 million environment steps in its experiments. MuZero demonstrated planning with a learned model across board games and Atari. These are later experiments if TinyCiv profiling and learning curves show that experience is the bottleneck and a learned model or lookahead can address it. [DreamerV3](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08744-2), [MuZero](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265) |

PPO is a practical first algorithm for the small, discrete action space. Mask impossible actions when sampling and when recomputing PPO log probabilities and entropy. Reusing the rollout mask is essential because legality changes with the state. Treat each selected posture as a command to a closed-loop planner, which can keep handling movement and threats throughout the action window. [PPO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347), [Invalid action masking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14171), [Temporal abstraction with options](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/classes/cs294/f05/papers/sutton%2Bal-1999.pdf)

## What TinyCiv already provides

- The headless runner freezes the production JS bundle and WASM. It calls `updateGame` with 0.1-second steps and a simulated wall clock. The production tick still has TypeScript orchestration around Rust systems. The current `sim_advance` advances the simulation clock, so a native trainer binding around that function would exercise only part of the game. [Harness guide](simulation-harness.md), [bundle entry points](../tools/simulation-harness/engine.mjs#L11), [production update](../src/game/simulation.ts#L3331), [native clock export](../rust/src/lib.rs#L1764)
- The ordinary strategy planner already asks Rust for garrison, retreat, recovery, hunt and campaign decisions. Its formation executor and the independent Officer Corps controller use their own admission rules. A new Rust policy resource can supply tactical overrides to the existing decisions while the engine continues to execute legal missions. The current strategy bridge is the place to collect the already-computed facts, and the existing policy dispatcher is the place to consume the override. [Strategy planner](../src/game/faction-strategy.ts#L142), [strategy cadence and Offensive priority](../src/game/faction-strategy.ts#L418), [Rust policy dispatcher](../rust/src/sim/ai_policy.rs#L190), [Officer Corps planner](../rust/src/sim/offensive_ai.rs#L46)
- The match runner already mirrors starting sides and reads the production defeated flag. In the archived 24-seed field study, `evolved-assault` went 24 wins, 20 draws and 4 losses against `expansionist`, and 21 wins, 21 draws and 6 losses against `hard-assault`. `field-concentration` was close to `evolved-assault` in the round robin, and their direct recovery-rule comparison was inconclusive. All 36 fortified transfer matches ended without a defeat or a siege engine present at the end. The generated-world comparison covered one map. These results establish field controls and show where the existing evaluation needs additional scenarios. [Iteration 2 results](../benchmarks/ai-iteration-2/README.md), [match runner](../tools/simulation-harness/match.mjs#L82)

## First controller

**One faction, one tactical action every two simulated seconds.** Each environment step advances 20 ordinary 0.1-second updates, stopping early on a game-rule outcome. All controlled factions observe the same completed world tick and submit actions before the next tick. The Rust resource holds each faction’s action for the window and applies its effect only when the relevant strategy decision runs. Keep scheduled emergency checks, home defense and Officer Corps precedence. Use the same observation, decision cadence and action application when the controller is embedded in the game.

| Action | Effect through the current planner |
| --- | --- |
| Default | Use the faction’s scripted branches. Always legal, including while its Leader is awaiting respawn. |
| Concentrate | Reserve zero *eligible healthy* escorts for the standing home garrison. The existing assignment still keeps unavailable or wounded soldiers out of the field escort. |
| Keep a reserve | Raise the reserve to at least one third of ready soldiers, rounded up and capped at the number of ready soldiers. |
| Withdraw | Request the existing recovery mission and hold it for the current window while eligible. Use the normal refuge and campaign-abandonment logic. A home-defense order can preempt a voluntary withdrawal. |
| Resume | In recovery, allow the early-resumption rule already exercised by `evolved-assault`. Require at least 70% Leader HP, morale of 60, and observed enemy power at most 1.1 times friendly power. An emergency recovery decision takes precedence. |
| Concentrate and resume | Apply the concentration and early-resumption choices together when both are eligible. |

Mask actions using current eligibility, including whether a faction has a usable Leader, eligible soldiers, or an ordinary mission that can accept the action. Revalidate at the strategy pass because the world can change during the window. If an action loses eligibility, use the normal branches and record the sampled action, its effective override, and the reason for any fallback or preemption. Keep the sampled action and its original log probability in the rollout, since the world’s response includes revalidation. Track the fraction of windows with multiple choices, actual overrides and fallback rates. A high overall step rate can hide very few chances for the learner to influence play.

Version an observation of roughly 32 scalars and flags. Start with Leader HP fraction, morale, respawn status, army composition and readiness, nearby friendly and visible hostile power, observed towers, home pressure, formation and mission state, distance home, recent contact, and time remaining when the scenario has a true time-limit rule. Normalize counts and power, use relative distances, and encode missing observations explicitly. Establish which facts are public and which require sight before exposing them. Check both values and masks by changing hidden enemy units or HP while keeping the faction-visible state fixed. Keep entity IDs, map seeds and opponent model IDs in replay metadata. Calculate any extra observation fields on the decision clock using existing indexed reads and cached values.

Start with a two-layer MLP with 64 units per layer, six masked actor logits and a training-only value head. The actor is about 6,700 weights and biases with 32 inputs, or about 26 KiB of raw `f32` weights. Recent contact and current mission state give the first model short-term context. Add a small recurrent layer only if tests on fog and interrupted engagements show that this representation loses decisive information.

## Harness contract and code placement

```text
Python learner and evaluator
    ↕ batched reset / step / close
Persistent Node workers running the frozen production bundle
    ↕ complete 0.1 s ticks and coarse decision boundaries
Rust / WASM faction facts, masks, action validation and policy inference
```

| Operation | Contract |
| --- | --- |
| `reset(episode_spec)` | Create a fresh world from a generated start or a frozen prepared scenario through the existing loader. Record generator seed, simulation seed, fixture hash, sides, opponents, time cap and rules. Return initial observations, masks and an episode/decision token. |
| `step(token, actions)` | Validate token, faction and slots against the published masks. Stage all actions together, run the fixed tick loop with an injected clock and return features, masks, reward terms, elapsed simulation ticks, `terminated`, `truncated` and execution diagnostics. Retain the completed transition’s observation when an episode ends. |
| `close(episode)` | Release native simulation handles and host-owned views deterministically. Duplicate close should be harmless and stale actions should be rejected. |

1. **Headless session.** Add the session under [the simulation harness](../tools/simulation-harness/). Extract reusable match setup and outcome handling from [`match.mjs`](../tools/simulation-harness/match.mjs#L9). Export only the additional entry points from [`engine.mjs`](../tools/simulation-harness/engine.mjs#L11). Use [the worker’s wall-clock and RNG guards](../tools/simulation-harness/worker.mjs#L5). Keep the existing round robin as the independent evaluator and add a neural entrant that loads the exported artifact.
2. **Rust policy resource.** Add a tactical policy module under `rust/src/sim` and a typed WASM bridge next to the existing policy API. Rust owns feature encoding, masks, overrides and action validity. Read the observation without running the mutating planner, and consume the override inside the normal garrison and recovery branches. The planner can already draft levies and change campaign state, so it must execute only once per due pass. Store the action separately from the registered profile code, which the host uses to decide when to reset strategy memory. Keep controller state keyed by faction and simulation handle. Compare scripted-only and forced-Default traces to check parity. [Planner side effects](../src/game/faction-strategy.ts#L183), [profile changes and strategy memory](../src/game/faction-strategy.ts#L420), [recurring work schedule](../rust/src/sim/work.rs#L1)
3. **Persistent worker and lifecycle.** Bundle once, import once per worker and initially run one active episode per worker. Exchange numeric arrays in a versioned, length-prefixed batch protocol over local pipes. Batch across workers on the learner side. Introduce explicit disposal in [`SimCore`](../src/game/sim-handle.ts#L27), coordinating its finalizers and invalidating native views. A fresh episode should recreate or reset worker-local transient state. A prepared save defines a start under the normal loader, and a mid-match save can omit transient planner or event state. Verify replay parity before using one for exact branching. [Native save sections](../rust/src/sim/mod.rs#L353), [event queue rebuild](../src/game/simulation-events.ts#L5)
4. **Training adapter.** Begin with a Gymnasium environment for a single learning faction against scripted opponents. A fixed `Discrete(6)` space is sufficient for SB3-Contrib MaskablePPO as a reference. Its recurrent policies are unsupported and subprocess masks must live in the environment. For behavior-cloning initialization or a batched collector with frozen opponents, use a small PyTorch PPO loop modeled on CleanRL and verify its losses against the reference on saved trajectories. The worker protocol already supports simultaneous actions when both factions are learned. [MaskablePPO](https://sb3-contrib.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo_mask.html), [CleanRL](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v23/21-1342.html), [PettingZoo Parallel API](https://pettingzoo.farama.org/api/parallel/)

Keep the fast path proportional to indexed records actually visited. Maintain faction counts and combat or economy accumulators where events already occur. Use bounded local queries, reuse fixed buffers, and share visibility and navigation results within their valid revisions. Avoid a separate terrain scan, full entity serialization, per-target path search or per-unit model call in the rollout loop. For a future target-ranking head, admit at most eight candidates from native indexed planners with stable tie-breaking, then score those rows in one batch with the same network. This keeps cost bounded when late-game factions have many units or cities. [Performance guidance](../skills/performance.md)

Save a manifest with the frozen bundle and WASM hashes, feature/action/reward schema versions, episode specs, seeds, runtime versions, learner settings, opponent and checkpoint hashes, and evaluation split. The learner needs the rollout observation, mask, action, behavior log probability, value, reward, ending flags and model version. Store counters and representative action traces for diagnosis. Replays should identify the first differing tick or decision and compare a canonical state digest on the same engine build. If a worker crashes, discard or deterministically replay its uncommitted segment and record the failure. Treat an unexpected pause, observer game over or unresolved human capture prompt as an environment error.

## Training stages

| Stage | Starts and opponents | Evidence to advance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Environment checks | Use the current field fixture, a short scenario with an achievable objective, and paired action traces. Run Default-only, legal random, and scripted contextual action selection. | Identical trace on fresh and reused workers. Confirm each action can take effect, masks survive edge cases including respawn, terminal rewards match the game outcome, and resident memory stabilizes across resets. |
| Tactical learning | Use generated terrain and canonical settled starts near contact. Vary force balance, composition, terrain, reinforcements, Leader health, and threat to the home city. Include offensive and defensive starts. Train against `expansionist`, `hard-assault`, `field-concentration` and `evolved-assault`. | Beat the strongest scripted controls in validation, including the best fixed posture and the exact unmodified full policy. Report decision and outcome changes per matchup. |
| Historical self-play | Sample one opponent at episode reset. As a starting mix use 50% scripted controls, 25% recent frozen checkpoints and 25% older or difficult frozen checkpoints. Rebalance based on full matchup results. Use the same base rules and observation scope for both sides. | The learner beats diverse archived policies and retains its performance across scenario families. Use the payoff matrix to expose cycles that a single Elo score can hide. [AlphaStar league](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphastar-grandmaster-level-in-starcraft-ii-using-multi-agent-reinforcement-learning/), [Policy-Space Response Oracles](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.00832) |
| Siege and field artillery | Use prepared Musketry and procedural towns with arrived or deployable artillery, reachable approaches, and normal replenishment. Check that a competent script can make progress and win under the chosen horizon. Add army objective or launch decisions at the Officer Corps seam and reuse its route and command validation. | Measured artillery arrival, gate breaches and city captures, plus actual victories across diverse maps. Keep established tactical wins in a regression suite. [Prepared Musketry](simulation-harness.md), [native Offensive planner](../rust/src/sim/offensive_ai.rs#L46) |
| Empire strategy | Use generated early, mid- and late-game starts, preserving settlements, exploration, raids and economy. Sample slower decisions through the Rust empire and investment planners. Expose legal priorities such as settle, build up industry, reinforce, or prepare a campaign. | Long matches show improved authoritative outcomes and acceptable economy, defense and simulation cost. Report separately by map family and era. [Empire planner](../rust/src/sim/ai_empire.rs#L82), [investment planner](../rust/src/sim/ai_investment.rs#L27) |

Use scripted demonstrations if plain PPO spends most of its budget rediscovering sensible tactics. Log the actual scripted garrison and recovery outputs and map them to equivalent legal actions at the same observation boundary. Exclude examples with no equivalent and measure label coverage. A teacher that emits Default in every state teaches delegation. Compare direct scripted play, cloning alone, PPO from scratch, and cloning followed by PPO. Use the same online rollout budget for the PPO arms and report demonstration collection cost separately. If cloning drifts in states the learner visits, query a trusted script there and add its labels. A live scripted opening with a scheduled handoff is also an option when later-start snapshots or labels are unavailable. [RAISocketAI cloning study](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.08112v2), [DAgger](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html), [Jump-Start RL](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/uchendu23a.html)

For the first PPO run, use a synchronous batch of around 2,000 transitions per update, for example 16 environments advancing 128 windows each. Start with a learning rate of `3e-4`, PPO clip range of `0.2` and GAE lambda of `0.95` as pilot settings. Log training and validation outcomes against environment transitions and wall-clock time, together with value error, entropy over legal actions, KL, draw rate and effective action use. Run a simple linear scorer on the same features as a control for whether the hidden layers add value.

### Rewards and time limits

Start tactical duels with +1 for winning, −1 for losing and 0 for a draw. Use the authoritative faction-defeated flag. A Leader loss may be followed by respawn, so record it as an event and let the game determine defeat. For targeted training tasks, define observable objectives such as surviving a defense or taking a specified city, with their own episode type and metrics. Keep broad match evaluation tied to faction defeat. [Match outcomes](../tools/simulation-harness/match.mjs#L91), [Leader respawn](../src/game/simulation.ts#L2892)

If decisive events are rare, shorten the route to contact using authentic starts and retain normal rules. If learning still stalls, ablate a small bounded potential using army and objective health advantage, `γ × Φ(next) − Φ(current)`. Use zero potential at true terminal states and the same discount as the value target. A per-kill, gold or construction bonus can make farming the proxy profitable while the faction loses. Log reward components and compare the policy with and without shaping using the same win-based evaluator. [Policy invariance under reward transformations](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pabbeel/cs287-fa09/readings/NgHaradaRussell-shaping-ICML1999.pdf)

For a duel defined to end in a draw at its limit, expose time remaining and set `terminated` at the limit. For an administrative cap on a longer campaign, set `truncated`, bootstrap from the final observation, and stop the advantage recursion across reset. The match report can still score a timed-out campaign as a draw. Choose discount according to simulated time. At a two-second interval, `γ = 0.99` has a half-life of about 138 simulated seconds and weights a one-hour outcome by about `1.4 × 10⁻⁸`. Select a longer horizon for empire decisions and carry time-based discounting into shaping and value targets if decision intervals vary. [Time Limits in RL](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/pardo18a.html), [Gymnasium ending semantics](https://farama.org/Gymnasium-Terminated-Truncated-Step-API)

Start training with a smoke budget in the tens of thousands of transitions and a tactical learning trial of 0.5–2 million per configuration. At an illustrative measured aggregate rate of 100 transitions/s, one million takes about 2.8 hours of collection before learner updates and evaluation. Use actual learning curves to decide whether to fund a longer run. Favor short tactics and prepared siege starts for collection and sample full campaigns primarily for transfer until profiling improves their cost.

If round trips matter, move collection to the persistent workers using a frozen Rust actor and emit rollout chunks to Python. Keep one behavior version per PPO collection round and use a controller RNG stream separate from the simulation RNG. If CPU simulation dominates, profile the production phases and optimize those first. A native Rust host is worth revisiting when it can run the complete production schedule with parity. If asynchronous actors later become necessary, measure policy lag and use an algorithm that accounts for it. [Sample Factory](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/petrenko20a.html), [IMPALA and V-trace](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561)

## Evaluation and deployment

- **Hold out worlds and policies.** Separate training, tuning and final generator seeds. Keep all descendants of a start in the same split. Also hold out whole geography or play-style families where feasible. Use mirror legs for each seed, plus same-policy mirror runs to measure side and update-order effects. Preserve normal geography, reachability, city placement and movement. The existing uniform field fixture has value as a deterministic control. [Existing fixture](../tools/simulation-harness/match.mjs#L9), [Procgen](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/cobbe20a.html)
- **Measure outcomes and uncertainty.** Publish wins, draws and losses, the score `(wins + 0.5 × draws) / games`, time to victory, and tactical or economic diagnostics per scenario and opponent. Group mirror legs by scenario and seed when bootstrapping confidence intervals. Train at least three independent seeds for a confirmation study and report their variability. Start validation with 64–128 paired seeds per key comparison and use the observed draw rate and seed variance to size the frozen final study. [Existing paired study](../benchmarks/ai-iteration-2/README.md), [RL evaluation under uncertainty](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/hash/f514cec81cb148559cf475e7426eed5e-Abstract.html)
- **Define promotion before tuning.** An initial tactical target is at least 55% draw-adjusted score against `evolved-assault` on held-out organic field starts, with the lower 95% confidence bound above 50%. Also require a predeclared noninferiority margin against `field-concentration`, the other top field policy. Report results against the remaining controls and fixed postures. Require enough decisive games to interpret the result and check defense, siege and full-world slices before broader deployment. Inspect cases where expected reward rises but wins fall, especially stalling, disbanding the garrison during raids, repeatedly switching missions, and ignoring siege objectives.
- **Ship the small actor in Rust/WASM.** Export bounded `f32` weights, feature ordering, normalization constants and schema/hash metadata. Verify Python and Rust/WASM logits and masked action choices on a shared golden observation set. Use stable tie-breaking and evaluate the same deterministic action mode intended for release. Load the model through the existing faction-policy selection path and use the scripted policy if the artifact fails to load or produces invalid output. The release budget should include observation extraction and action admission. An initial target is ≤1 ms p95 of total added work in a frame with multiple AI decisions on the reference browser device, within TinyCiv’s 33 ms frame budget. Reuse buffers and profile synchronized factions and worst-case populations. [Faction policy bridge](../src/game/faction-ai-profile.ts#L176), [Performance guidance](../skills/performance.md)

The first implementation milestone consists of a deterministic reset/step/replay path, one Rust tactical action head, an end-to-end masked PPO run, and a mirrored evaluation report against the scripted controls. Its measurements determine whether to expand the action space or to invest first in faster simulation and better training starts.
