Design notes

Game requirements:
- RTS
- Top down 2D visuals
- Implemented in typescript / javascript
- Use 3rd party libs when possible
- Art style is 2D / pixel art style. Think Game Body Advance Wars meets Civilization 3. Art style could also be very similar to Rimworld.
- World design is base tile type (ocean/coast/river/lake/desert/plains/grassland/tundra) with overlays (rocks / hills / mountains / forest / jungle / swamp / volcano / glacier)
- Tiles should have realistic looking interfaces between tile types and not have sharp straight lines.
- Each tile type should have at least one subtle animation like a small wave on the coast, an ocean sparkle, waving small patch of grass, trees gently swaying, smoke cloud coming from volcano, ripple in swamp.
- The art style should be relatively bright and cheery, with simple colors, i.e. like Super Mario
- Try to find open source 2d sprites with animations; you may edit them slightly. 
- For world generation we should use a patch-resampling approach (e.g. multipoint statistics approach) which uses small patches of real world world data (augmented with rotations) and stitches them in by affinity/overlap. This will produce realistic-looking features like coastlines, mountain ranges, and so on.
- User controls the civilization’s Leader, represented as a person sprite wearing a gold crown. WASD controls to move him; within the viewport, the player can click and interact with objects. For enemies within range, the Leader can attack. He is equipped with a stone spear to start. He starts with a retinue of two Warriors who follow him and automatically attack nearby targets within a range of 8 tiles of the Leader. If the Leader leaves the area, the retinue follows him. They can be commanded to stay by clicking on one of them; they will then stay until the player clicks on them and summons them back
- Units all occupy a single pixel; they should have attack and movement animations akin to those in Warcraft: Orcs and Humans or Warcraft 2
- World should be randomly generated to start, with continents realistically generated using similar algorithms to civilization. There should be patches of forest, bands of mountains flanked with hills, deserts with varying topography, and equatorial rainforests. There should be rivers running into the ocean or into/from lakes which can be small to very large
- Military unit have an attack/defense/speed/morale/range rating
- Civilizations should be placed randomly, but with a minimum buffer separating them.
- Population system is as follows: there is a housing progression system similar to Caesar 3 and other city building games; levels of housing are Camp -> Hovel -> Shack -> Shanty -> Cottage -> House -> Townhouse -> Tenement -> Apartment -> Highrise. For now, just implement Camp, Shack, Cottage. Each of them can support 1 additional population. Each one takes up one grid square. Camps host a single population point. Houses can upgrade when 1) technology is advanced enough, and 2) they have access to the requisite resources. Each population point requires housing; if their housing is destroyed, they disappear from the game UNLESS there is empty housing/open population slot somewhere in the civ which they can move to 
- Player starts with 5 camps scattered within a 5 tile radius of the start
- For every 10 population, an extra Warrior is added to retinue. When a retinue unit dies, a population point is lost. Each population also has a working location; farmers use an open field near their home and produce yield just like in civ 3 (food / hammers / wealth). Game will automatically place work location in optimal place nearby (within X tiles of their home) though workers can be manually placed at new locations by selecting them and having them move to a new location. Tiles should show food/hammer/gold yields in small faint icons on the tile. Workers will be permanently on the tile; they do not move back to their home. They can be attacked by enemy units. If this happens, they die and the population will need to be regenerated.
- Military units should show a small, segmented health bar (like starcraft units) above them when they are damaged. When they are out of combat (>30 seconds) and near their leader, they should slowly regenerate. All units have 100 health max.
- Population growth occurs when there is surplus food; it happens slowly when there is little surplus and faster when there is a lot. If there are no open homes for population to appear in, a worker will move to a new tile and start a new home. They will try to immediately build the most advanced house type they can, given the resources and technology level.
- Nearby there should be scattered enemy camps under the same game system (housing, food, etc). Each enemy faction should have an Affinity score which captures how culturally and linguistically similar they are. Each faction has a Palace building (2x2 building). When an opposing Leader approaches it, the player can click on the building to open diplomacy, and declare war / ask for confederation. Confederation is a random chance; with some probability it succeeds or fails. If it fails, there is a chance they will declare war immediately. These success chances depend on the affinity score. 
- Nearby enemy camps with low affinity scores may start the game at war with the player; they will occasionally send raiding parties to attack the player and attempt to destroy the palace
- When military units destroy buildings belonging to another faction, they win gold for their faction. Killing opposing enemy units will also yield a smaller amount of gold.
- When units have very low health, their morale will rapidly decrease. They should show a small, slightly shaking exclamation mark in white hovering over the top of their unit. When morale hits zero, they flee. The morale system should work similarly to the total war series; it recovers when there are nearby units fighting, and it recovers quickly when nearby enemies rout or die. It also recovers faster when the leader is nearby. If there is a substantial overmatch in enemy forces nearby, it decreases. While they are routing, they will flee to the nearest friendly buildings. Fleeing units will move slightly slower (5% slower) than normal units, so units which are chasing them can occasionally land hits.
- There should also be a few stray animals (deer, buffalo, elephant, ostrich)  which the Leader can kill, providing a small food bonus

- When the player presses B, features can be placed within a 10 tile radius of the leader. These include walls and roads. Walls obstruct enemies and can take a lot of damage; roads can speed up movement. Farms get a small food production bonus from being within 5 tiles of a road that is connected to the palace.
- In UI, the food/gold/hammer production should be shown in top left; the amount generated every 30 seconds should be shown like (+2). Maintaining retinue costs gold, maintaining buildings costs hammers.
- Far away from any factions, there should be small ruins (show a small house with a broken roof and a crumbling wall) which the Leader can move onto and claim for a small randomly selected bonus of either gold or hammers
- For now, do not implement a tech tree
