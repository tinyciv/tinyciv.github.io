# Relief art direction

Use the accepted blocky pixel style: coarse diagonal sides, short rounded or flat-round hilltops, and three broad grassy bands. Keep upper-left lighting, hard pixel edges, and a consistent native pixel scale. Vary widths and silhouettes without adding fine texture, gradients, or extra shading bands.

## Range composition

1. Build wide, overlapping ranges and clumps with a substantial body.
2. Keep every hill attached to its range. No isolated hills or detached satellite bumps.
3. Allow a single-hill cross-section only at the very northern or southern terminus. Broaden promptly into the range body.
4. Enclose the large mountain core within surrounding hills and foothills.
5. Offset neighboring hill midpoints in both axes. Use soft, irregular staggering rather than aligned rows or columns.

## Variation

- Vary range geometry with bends, asymmetric shoulders, and broad clumps while retaining connected bodies.
- Let width and hill size change along each range instead of repeating a constant profile.
- Mix mountain silhouettes and clustered rocky exposure; vary the length, density, and gaps of enclosed cores.
- Retain the original mountain sprites with grassy hill bodies and rocky summits. New variants supplement these combined shapes.
- Leave room between successive north–south layers so individual silhouettes read clearly, while retaining continuous overlap through the range.

## Review checklist

- The body reads wider than a single-file chain of hills.
- Overlapping silhouettes form connected ranges without exposed stamp lattices or broad horizontal cuts.
- Single-hill caps occur only at the northern and southern ends and remain connected to their range.
- Large mountains sit inside an enclosing hill mass.
- Centers stagger in both axes; shoulders and edges taper irregularly.
