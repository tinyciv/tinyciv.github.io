# Art and asset provenance

The game primarily uses **Shade's Puny asset family**, licensed **CC0 1.0**. The source atlas and character PNGs remain unchanged. Runtime rendering selects and joins source tiles, composes roads and native gates, recolors clothing, and adds faction chest cloth. Hills and mountains use Ansimuz’s Tiny Overworld; rocks use the LPC Rocks collection. Additional active artwork includes farm art and supplied HUD references. Generated timber palace and wooden settlement studies are retained as archived comparison assets; the game no longer loads them. These separate assets retain their own terms below and are not covered by Shade’s CC0 declaration. No Warcraft or Kenney assets are bundled.

- [Creator's OpenGameArt listing and CC0 license declaration](https://opengameart.org/content/16x16-puny-world-tileset).

Its ground matches native sand `#c9b266`; the stone uses a warmer nine-shade ramp. Every source pixel position, alpha value, corner and cardinal stair is preserved. Grassland/tundra ledges retain the original atlas colors. Both sheets load once, and the cached terrain raster selects the sheet per tile; joining and movement rules are unchanged. The review gallery and exact palette mappings are in `scratch/2026-10-28-desert-ledges/`.
- Rivers descending south-facing ledges use approved **1A — Original Puny waterfall** from `scratch/2026-11-04-waterfall-ledges/gallery.html`: four complete 16×16 cells starting at `(64,352)`, stepping 16 pixels right, in the original 100 ms frame order. Connected reaches use the original banked river tiles, preserving the full ten-pixel water opening and rocky waterfall flanks. `src/game/banked-river-art.ts` selects these unchanged source shapes; desert banks reuse the approved desert-ledge palette while water and alpha remain unchanged. No generated artwork or new source PNGs are used.
- Used for grass, dirt, shores, water, trees, ford pebbles, harvest stockpiles, buildings, construction sites, roads, walls, bridges, and HUD construction pictures.
- Beaches and inland sand share the native warm sand rim `(352,0,48,48)` and inward corners `(400,0,32,32)`. Sandy shore water uses `(192,160+48f,48,48)` with inward corners `(240,160+32f,32,32)`; grass shore water uses the corresponding blocks at x112 and x160. The native turquoise-to-cyan and cyan-to-deep-blue transition rims are at x272 and x352, y`160+48f`, with inward corners at x320 and x400. Here `f` is animation frame 0–3. Runtime selects these tiles by distance from land; it never recolors the source pixels or applies gradients.
- Coastal palms use the original single-trunk tree at `(48,448,16,16)`, without recoloring or altered proportions. `src/game/palm-art.ts` draws it at the same native pixel scale as the surrounding terrain. Beach scenery uses no generated or third-party artwork.
- `src/game/puny-world.ts` loads the atlas; terrain, scenery, and road renderers select and join its source tiles. Rock, hill, and mountain overlays use the separate reviewed packs detailed below.
- River fords use three broad stone fragments cropped from the original Puny pebble cluster at `(19,425,7,5)`. `src/game/ford-art.ts` draws them at the terrain's native scale (14×10 world pixels each), aligned across the opposite banks. Two retain the source olive palette; the center stone maps those four shades to existing Puny tan wall colors. These are composed fragments, not a dedicated ford sprite supplied by the pack. Transparent gaps reveal the original animated water. The source atlas stays unchanged. A standalone comparison with native-green and warmer alternatives is in `scratch/2026-10-27-ford-stones/gallery.html`.
- Fields have no stationary farming figures. Fields have three connected stages: short light-green seedlings, taller green crops, and the purchased PixelHole golden wheat; waiting grain uses its wooden crate. Carried provisions retain the compact Puny provision crate. Farm plots have no decorative fencing.
- Settlement buildings use the original World atlas; the stone monument uses the matching original Dungeon statue described below. Housing level 1 uses the tent at (4,26), level 2 the straight/flat-roof house at (4,27), and levels 3–4 the gable house at (5,27). Coordinates here are zero-based 16-pixel cells. Wells bring nearby level-1 housing to level 2 without stacking; higher levels retain their artwork. The static covered well uses the native narrow variant at (5,30), source rectangle (80,480,16,16).
- `src/game/puny-building-art.ts` defines source rectangles and caches faction variants by source and color. A pixel-by-pixel comparison of the gold, cyan and red atlas copies identifies team-color regions. Only those regions are recolored, keeping the original alpha, neutral wood, stone, outlines, and discrete shading. World buildings, placement previews, fog memories and HUD icons share this path. No faction IDs are hard-coded into art selection.
- The palace uses two native 16×32 columns side by side: `(192,416,16,32)` and `(208,416,16,32)`, forming the complete 32×32 castle composition at 2× world scale. Both source rows are preserved. The creator’s sample maps verify this arrangement; the former lower-half crop and improvised stone court have been removed. Wooden towers use the raised platform at (7,27) on original timber posts; tower art keeps its 1×1 footing and 24-world-pixel overhang.
- Buildable walls now use the native stone pieces at (18–20,26–29). Straight horizontal/vertical runs and all four corners follow the original sample-map layout; T/cross junctions use the full native closed pier so adjoining cells meet. Only completed, same-faction walls connect; placement ghosts connect before construction. The construction menu calls this **Wall**, with the existing 6 gold / 225 HP / armor 2 / 4-second balance.
- Stone wall and wooden palisade ends also join adjacent cliff edges using the unchanged straight sprites. `src/game/wall-cliffs.ts` clips the extension against the native ledge silhouette, covering approach-side ground shadows so it meets the rock without crossing onto the summit. Long corner fringes stop at the central wall body's contact depth, avoiding pointed timber tails; authored one-pixel variation on straight shoulders remains intact. Grass, desert, both elevation tiers and placement previews share this treatment; stairs remain open. This changes artwork only, with no additional wall entities or collision changes. Standalone before/after comparisons are `output/cliff-wall-joins.html` and `output/cliff-corner-joins.html`.
- Gates use the complete original open stone arch at `(304,464,16,16)`, including its authored dark doorway and overhead lintel, with roads behind it. The closed-door and barred variants are at `(288,464,16,16)` and `(320,464,16,16)`. Gates remain passable to their owner and confederated allies; artwork does not decide access. No flags or pennants are added to buildings, defenses, bridges, construction sites, farms or HUD pictures. Faction recoloring applies only to the color regions already present in the native building sprites. Color lookup follows faction IDs and stored colors, including reordered faction arrays.
- The supplied collection at `scratch/2026-10-26-puny-world-collection/` contains a byte-identical official World atlas and recovered original sample maps. Exact source/map coordinates, hashes and reference sheets are recorded in `scratch/2026-10-26-puny-walls-palace/inventory.json`; production examples are in that folder’s `gallery.html`. No atlas PNG was modified.
- Diagonally adjacent traffic roads connect through their shared corners using the same native dirt pathway samples as cardinal road segments.
- Dirt trails reuse `src/game/road-sprites.ts` and the same Puny straight-path source cell `(48,16,16,16)`. Their nominal half-width is 65% of the standard road’s; source dirt RGB channels are multiplied by `(0.91,0.86,1.03)` and rounded to a slightly darker, warmer palette. The native grass shoulder `(157,167,71)` is preserved, and background grass remains transparent. Sixteen stable variants add gentle interior curves, modest width changes and different texture samples, chosen by map coordinates for both village paths and camp tracks. A three-world-pixel border band stays fixed to preserve cardinal and diagonal connections. Both render caches include the bounded variant ID; the original atlas and ordinary road artwork are unchanged. Comparisons are in `output/road-comparison.html` and `output/dirt-trail-variety.html`.
- `src/game/building-ground.ts` samples the native dirt path pixels into tan clearings beneath solid buildings. A procedural alpha mask feathers the perimeter into the existing neighboring terrain; shoreline clipping keeps the clearing off water. This is runtime compositing of Puny texture, not a separate painted terrain asset.
- All construction sites use four small wooden pegs from the original atlas to mark their footprint until completion. A small circular countdown in the center appears after construction work begins, shows remaining work as a shrinking pie, and freezes when work is interrupted. Footprints awaiting their first builder have no countdown. There is no timber platform, lumber, linear progress bar, or temporary dirt clearing. Attached wells offset both pegs and countdown to their front-left position.
- A well sharing a house tile uses approved overlay **2A** from `scratch/2026-10-27-house-well-overlays/gallery.html`: the complete narrow well at the original pixel scale, offset (-6,+3) native pixels / (-12,+6) world pixels to the front left. Houses and wells keep independent ownership-matched entities. The renderer draws the well in front regardless of build order, and both remain individually selectable. Standalone wells use the same narrow sprite centered in their tile.
- A wooden tower sharing a wall tile uses the existing tower sprite, drawn after the joined wall in either build order. The upper tower and lower wall face have separate selection areas. Towers on active or completed walls omit the standalone dirt clearing. Proposed shorter roofed, open and masonry tops remain a separate study in `scratch/2026-10-27-wall-tower-options/gallery.html`; none of those new top compositions is selected yet.

## Irrigation

`public/assets/irrigation/` contains the selected slim 1B ditch, matching river inlet, and a cropped Miniworld wooden bridge. Ditches use the purchased Puny World Medieval river's four original animation frames, compressed to a four-pixel footprint on a native 16×16 tile; these water assets use the purchased project license, not CC0. The bridge is the unchanged `(42,11,28,36)` sprite from Miniworld's `Miscellaneous/Bridge.png`. Source rectangles, frame layouts, and connection masks are recorded in `source.json`.

The runtime compresses that original four-pixel sheet to a three-pixel channel footprint, preserves its four animation frames, and tapers the inlet to match.
